# The resident stories

Eleven residents, each lost their balance in their region's way; rests at the region's spots move their story on, and when it ends their apprentice joins as a playable folk (`cast.md`). The data is [art/story/stories.json](../../art/story/stories.json); this page is a readable copy of it on 21 Oct, so the JSON wins if they ever differ. A proposal: nothing here is in the game until Matt says yes ([choices-for-matt.md](choices-for-matt.md)).

## How a story moves on

- **Chapter 1** comes with the first rest at any of the region's rest spots, of any length.
- **Chapters 2 and 3** each need one more rest there that ends on a later day than the chapter before, or a long rest (90 minutes or more; a night counts). One chapter per rest, and at most one new chapter a day in all (a proposal, like one way a day).
- A chapter is the beat in the next welcome-back ("while you were away ...") and stays in the journal. Nothing waits on a date, nothing expires, nothing is lost, and no one gets worse while the player is away. No chapter needs a walk, a fetch, a payment or a fight.
- **Chapter 3** leaves a lasting change in the world (presentation only) and the apprentice asks to come along; they join at once and their stone in the woodland circle lights.

## How these were chosen

Matt asked for content to be drafted in several versions and graded independently. Three drafters each wrote all eleven stories from one brief, each with its own approach: **A**, one object in three states (a kettle, a watch, a star chart); **B**, the apprentice sees it first and helps the resident stop; **C**, rest is contagious (the resident catches the companion's naps). Three graders scored all 33 drafts blind to each other, each on five criteria out of 10: **player and voice** (charm, distinct voices, arcs that read days apart, an earned ask, humour); **Matt's rules and cosiness**, adversarially (rest the only engine, nothing lost, no guilt or FOMO, honesty about what the game knows, decorative stays decorative); **world and cost** (fits the theme and the cast, real places, cheap world changes, set at the right spots, originality).

B led in nine regions outright, tied A on the fire country and trailed A on the isles. B is kept everywhere: for the fire country, A had Ashe carry off Hearthold's bedtime bell (which the atlas has still ringing) and B hands it to the smith; for the isles, B's Tuff and naturalist read better and its one conflict (the boulder seen at the headwaters) was a single line to fix, with A's best line grafted in. Then every grader finding was fixed:

- **Honesty.** No chapter 1 claims a night or a long time away ("all night", "at 3 a.m.", "dawn to dusk"), because chapter 1 can come from a five-minute rest; nothing says the player slept.
- **Rest, not labour.** Sunstone's creek no longer runs because the trader and Mirra dig the drift out; it runs because the trader finally leaves the pool alone and rests, and the water finds its own way (C's idea).
- **No lectures, no phone digs.** "the water's saying slow down", "that is progress" and "the water doesn't need to be filmed" are gone; the fire rival's "before the bell?" no longer puts a deadline on a spar.
- **Order-free.** No apprentice lights "the seventh stone": stories finish in whatever order the player rests.
- **Cheap to draw.** The valley's deckchair (a Driftwood prop) and second bench are gone; Cogsworth's minute-slow tower clock is a chime a minute late (sound) rather than a redrawn landmark nobody could read at pixel size.
- **Grafts** from the other drafts: "tastes better. don't tell the kettle" (A, Frostline), "it hums. in g" (A, isles), "i'm not charging for it. don't tell anyone" (C, Sunstone), "weirdly okay?" (A, Neon), "i'd recommend it to me" (C, Halcyon).

## The home valley: the weeds will keep

**The gardener**, and Cairn who joins. The gardener tends from dawn to lantern-light; a stone that has watched her for years wakes and teaches her to sit still till the moss grows.

**1. a stone turns round** (the hut's bed). while you were away the gardener was weeding again, beds, then borders, then the beds. your grassling dozed by the hut. in the woodland circle, one mossy stone seems to face the garden now.

> **gardener**: one more row. then the beans. then the row after the beans.  
> **Cairn**: ...i have watched her weed for ninety summers. well. nine. stones round up.  
> **companion**: did that rock just talk. it has a fern behind its ear.

**2. rain at the shelter** (the millpond bench). while you were away a shower came over the millpond. the gardener ducked under the shelter by the green and found three neighbours already there. a mossy stone stood at the end of the bench, steaming gently.

> **Cairn**: she sat. for the whole shower. i counted it in moss. a good half inch.  
> **gardener**: funny. i've built that shelter twice and never once sat in it.  
> **companion**: the rain did the watering for her. rain is kind like that.

_Stays changed:_ a kettle hangs on a hook in the shelter by the green, and neighbours gather there when it rains.

**3. sit still till the moss grows** (the bandstand in the park). while you were away the band played at dusk. cairn walked down from the circle, sat beside the gardener on the bandstand step and rested one mossy hand on her trowel. she let him keep it.

> **Cairn**: the weeds will keep. so will you. that is the whole of my wisdom.  
> **gardener**: it's a good tune. i never heard the end of it before.  
> **companion**: she's asleep. her hat's slipped. i won't tell her.  
> **Cairn**: i have stood still a long time. i think i'd like to sit still somewhere new.

_Stays changed:_ the gardener's trowel hangs on a hook in the shelter by the green, which is now a meeting place with a lantern; at dusk she sits there, not in the beds.

**Cairn asks:** "she has her shelter and her neighbours. may a slow stone walk with you a while?" Cairn steps out of his place in the woodland circle, leaving a ring of pale grass, and one more stone in the circle lights. he falls in a step behind you.

**For the map lane:** a kettle on a hook in the shelter by the green; the gardener seated on the bandstand step, hat slipped (pose); the trowel on a hook and a lit lantern in the shelter; Cairn's empty place in the circle: a ring of pale grass.

## The Brackens: forty-two in the fold

**The drover**, and Tally who joins. The drover walks his flock up and down the Drover's Road and never stops; his lamb Tally learns to bring them in, and he learns to sit on the bench and let her.

**1. counting on the move** (the shepherd's hut step). while you were away the drover's flock went out past the hut, then back past it, without stopping. a small lamb in a cardigan ran behind, notching a stick. your grassling slept on the step.

> **Tally**: forty-one, forty-two... wait, he's off again. i'll start over.  
> **drover**: can't stop. grass is greener at the gap. then greener back here.  
> **companion**: the dog looks tired. its tongue is out sideways.

**2. a drink at the drove well** (the drove well). while you were away the flock stopped at the drove well to drink. the dog lay down in the shade. the drover stood a long while, then leaned on the wall, then very nearly sat.

> **Tally**: i got them all counted. forty-two. it's easy when nobody's walking.  
> **drover**: dog's never lain down on a drove before. maybe it's the well.  
> **companion**: i think it's the lying down. lying down is nice.

_Stays changed:_ the drover's dog dozes in the shade of the drove well, belly up.

**3. the fold bell** (the bench below the Brackens gap). while you were away tally rang her fold bell at dusk and walked the flock in herself, one notch at a time. the drover sat on the bench below the gap and took his boots off.

> **Tally**: forty-two in. gate's shut. you can sit now. you're sitting. good.  
> **drover**: road'll still be there in the morning. never thought of it that way.  
> **companion**: his socks have holes. his toes look very happy about it.

_Stays changed:_ at dusk the sheepfold by the road holds the whole flock behind a shut gate, and the drover sits on the bench below the gap with his boots beside him.

**Tally asks:** "he's got the hang of sitting. could i come and count the miles with you? i'll go slow." Tally hangs a spare notched stick on the shepherd's hut for the drover, rings her fold bell once, and trots after you.

**For the map lane:** the drover's dog asleep belly-up by the drove well; the sheepfold with the flock inside and the gate shut; the drover seated on the bench below the gap, boots off beside him.

## The Lull plain, Lullford and Gullwick: the river runs downhill on its own

**The bargee**, and Puddle who joins. The bargee keeps old Clover pulling up and down the Lull year after year; his frog apprentice notices the river does half the work, and the horse gets a field.

**1. clover on the towpath** (Lullford's quay and mooring). while you were away the barge went down past lullford, then back up, then down. the old horse on the towpath plodded the whole way. a frog in red wellies sat on the mooring post and watched.

> **bargee**: walk on, clover. there's coal for the delta and bricks back up.  
> **Puddle**: clover's pulled since i was a tadpole. and i was a very long tadpole.  
> **companion**: the horse has a grey nose. grey noses need naps.

**2. under the willows** (the willows by the oxbow). while you were away the barge tied up under the willows at the oxbow. the bargee frowned at the time. clover put her head down in the long grass and would not lift it.

> **Puddle**: she's not stubborn. she's listening. the water's very quiet here.  
> **bargee**: never moored here before. didn't know the oxbow had this much shade.  
> **companion**: the willow is dipping its hair in the water. like a bath.

_Stays changed:_ clover grazes under the willows by the oxbow with the barge moored beside her, rope slack.

**3. the tide does the pulling** (a boat hauled up at Gullwick). while you were away puddle unhitched the towrope and let the ebb carry the barge down to the sea. the bargee walked clover along the coast and sat on a hauled-up boat at gullwick.

> **Puddle**: river goes downhill on its own. it never needed pulling that way.  
> **bargee**: forty years on that rope and the sea was just over the hill.  
> **companion**: clover is standing in the waves up to her knees. she looks very pleased.

_Stays changed:_ clover dozes in the meadow behind Lullford quay, her harness hung on the mooring post, and the barge drifts down with the tide.

**Puddle asks:** "clover's got her field and he's got the tide. fancy a frog who knows where the water goes?" Puddle tips the water out of his wellies, pats clover's grey nose goodbye, and hops into step beside you.

**For the map lane:** clover grazing under the oxbow willows, barge moored with a slack rope; clover at rest in the meadow behind Lullford quay; the harness hung over the mooring post.

## The Frostline: the kettle is always on

**The hut warden**, and Tam who joins; ring rival: the hut warden. The hut warden keeps the bothy open and the kettle on for every walker and never sits down; her marmot apprentice takes the kettle so she can drink a cup herself.

**1. always open** (the bothy's bunk). while you were away the hut warden swept the snow off the step four times, filled the kettle six times and never once sat down. a marmot in a bobble hat watched from the woodpile.

> **warden**: the bothy is always open. the kettle is always on. mind the step.  
> **Tam**: she makes tea for everyone. i've never seen her drink a cup. not one.  
> **companion**: the bunk is warm. i think she warmed it for us.

**2. two mugs at the col** (the Col hut bench). while you were away the warden came down to the col hut for bread. tam had poured two mugs and set one on the bench. the warden drank hers standing, but she drank it all.

> **Tam**: i put sugar in it. she said too much sugar. then she drank it all.

**3. tam's on kettle** (the tarn shore at Snowmelt Lake). while you were away tam kept the bothy for a whole evening. the warden walked down to the tarn, sat on a flat rock among the cotton grass, and watched the ice for the first time in years.

**Spar (optional):** the hut warden: "a friendly bout in the snow? afterwards we both sit. i'm told that's allowed now."

**For the map lane:** a dented mug upside down on the Col hut bench; the warden seated on the tarn shore rock (one seated pose); the warden seated on the bench by the bothy door, mug in hand.

## Cogsworth, the coal hills and the Narrows: a minute slow

**The tinkerer**, and Pendle who joins; ring rival: the tinkerer. The tinkerer runs every clock in town to the second; the automaton she once set a minute slow gives her that minute back.

**1. the eight-fourteen** (the allotments' bench, warm from the foundry). while you were away the tinkerer wound the tower clock, checked the station clock, and set the foundry's whistle by her pocket watch. a brass automaton on the platform called each train a little after it left.

> **tinkerer**: eight-fourteen and twelve seconds. no, thirteen. thirteen. right.  
> **Pendle**: the eight-fourteen is now arriving. ...it arrived a minute ago. i say these things after.  
> **companion**: this bench is warm. the foundry is breathing on it.

**2. a spare minute** (the narrowboat's cabin). while you were away the tinkerer came aboard the narrowboat to mend its clock. the boat was moving at walking pace through the lock. she sat down to wait for it, and pendle sat with her.

> **Pendle**: you set me a minute slow once. you said, keep this one for a rainy day.  
> **tinkerer**: did i. i don't remember. that does sound like something i'd want.  
> **Pendle**: ...i have kept it. it is still here. it is very well polished.  
> **companion**: the lock is filling up slowly. i like watching it.

_Stays changed:_ the narrowboat's cabin clock stands open with its hands taken off and set on the shelf beside it.

**3. the clock room chair** (the clock room). while you were away pendle climbed the tower and set the great clock a minute slow. the sunset chime came a minute after everyone expected. the tinkerer, in a chair by the clock room window, dozed right through it.

> **Pendle**: there is your minute. take as long as you like with it.  
> **tinkerer**: it's a minute slow. everything's a minute slow. and it's lovely.  
> **companion**: the clock goes tick. she goes snore. they take turns.

_Stays changed:_ the tower's sunset chime rings a minute after the station clock's (sound only), and the tinkerer naps in a chair by the clock room window at dusk.

**Pendle asks:** "...i would like to come with you. apologies. i decided that a minute ago." Pendle hands its conductor's whistle to the tinkerer, swings its pendulum once in goodbye, and boards with you a minute after the doors open.

**Spar (optional):** the tinkerer: "a bout in the plaza? starts on the chime. well, a minute after the chime."

**For the map lane:** the narrowboat's open cabin clock with its hands on a shelf; a chair by the clock room window with the tinkerer asleep in it (pose); the sunset chime a minute late (a sound cue for lane M, no art).

## The Tidebloom Isles: an inch a year

**The naturalist**, and Tuff who joins. The naturalist counts every crab, fern and wave and never just listens; a boulder that moves an inch a year teaches her to put the pencil behind her ear.

**1. two hundred and six crabs** (a hammock at Lagoon Landing). while you were away the naturalist counted the crabs on the pier, the ferns by the boathouse and the waves on the reef. by the cove, a big basalt boulder looks an inch nearer the path.

> **naturalist**: two hundred and six crabs. two hundred and seven. stay still, you.  
> **Tuff**: ......hm.  
> **companion**: that boulder has a turtle on it. the turtle is asleep. i like the turtle.

**2. pages too wet to write on** (the rain shelter at the headwaters). while you were away a long rain came over the headwaters. the naturalist's notebook went soft, so she sat in the rain shelter with nothing to count, and listened to the fall.

> **naturalist**: can't write. can't count. there's a frog going ploop somewhere. one frog.  
> **Tuff**: ...she heard it. ...good.  
> **companion**: the rain is so loud the frog has to say it twice. ploop. ploop.

_Stays changed:_ the naturalist's notebook hangs open to dry on the rain shelter's rail, pages fanned.

**3. a pencil behind the ear** (the black sand of the cove). while you were away the naturalist lay on the warm black sand beside the boulder for a whole afternoon. she didn't count the waves. the boulder told her what they sounded like.

> **Tuff**: ...listen. ...in. ...out. ...it hums. ...in g.  
> **naturalist**: i had them down as four hundred waves. they're really just the one, aren't they.  
> **companion**: her pencil is behind her ear. it looks like it lives there now.

_Stays changed:_ the naturalist lies on the black sand by a smooth dent where the boulder used to sit, notebook shut, pencil behind her ear.

**Tuff asks:** "...an inch a year is good. ...i would like to try a mile. ...with you. ...slowly." Tuff rises out of his dent in the sand, sea thrift nodding and the turtle still asleep on his back, and follows you at a pace he calls brisk.

**For the map lane:** the naturalist's notebook fanned on the rain shelter rail; a smooth boulder-shaped dent in the cove's black sand; the naturalist lying on the sand, pencil behind her ear.

## The Sunstone basin: the creek remembers

**The oasis trader**, and Mirra who joins; ring rival: the cowhand. The oasis trader ladles water to every passer-by and never lets the spring rest; when she finally leaves the pool alone, the buried outlet clears and the gulch's creek runs again.

**1. a cup for everyone** (the palm shade at Palm Spring). while you were away the trader ladled water under her awning, counting cups and coins. the pool sat low. a gecko with a forked rod kept pointing it at the drift by the outlet.

> **trader**: a cup for you, a cup for you, a cup for the camel. next.  
> **Mirra**: rod's twitching at that dune again. water's under there. old water.  
> **companion**: nobody gave the trader a cup. i'd give her one if i had hands.

**2. tea at the camp** (the caravan camp under the stars). while you were away the trader rolled up her awning at dusk and walked to the caravan camp. she drank tea by the fire with the camels, and mirra told her what the rod had found.

> **Mirra**: the creek didn't dry up. it got buried. there's a difference.  
> **trader**: i kept ladling faster so the pool wouldn't notice. it noticed.  
> **companion**: the stars are very big out here. the camels aren't looking at them.

_Stays changed:_ the trader's awning is rolled up at dusk, with a moon pictogram hung on its pole.

**3. water down the gulch** (the saloon's porch). while you were away the trader left the ladle in the pool and slept under the palms. in the quiet she heard water moving under the drift. by morning a thin creek ran past the saloon's porch.

> **Mirra**: told you. water knows the way. it only wanted the pool left alone a while.  
> **trader**: a glass of water. slowly. on a porch. i'm not charging for it. don't tell anyone.  
> **companion**: the creek is making a little song. i'm going to sit and hear all of it.

_Stays changed:_ a thin creek runs again down the gulch's old dry bed past the saloon, and the trader sits on the porch at dusk with a glass of water.

**Mirra asks:** "creek's found its way home. i'd like to go find a few more. room for a gecko?" Mirra leaves her spare rod leaning on the trader's awning pole, tips her wide hat, and walks out with you along the new creek.

**Spar (optional):** the cowhand: "corral's dusty and so am i. a friendly round, if you've a mind to it."

**For the map lane:** the trader's awning rolled up with a moon pictogram on its pole; water frames in the gulch's dry creek bed and a gap in the drift at the outlet; the trader seated on the saloon porch with a glass (pose).

## The fire country: banked at sunset

**The smith**, and Ashe who joins; ring rival: the smith. The smith of the Ember forge works through the night until her fire burns her out; the keeper of Hearthold's bedtime bell carries the bell to her, and she banks the forge at sunset.

> **Ashe**: i rang the bell. the whole castle went to bed. the forge did not.  
> **smith**: one more blade. the fire's hot now. it's a waste to let it cool.  
> **companion**: the bath is warm. the forge looks too warm.

**2. the hammer on the wall** (Hearthold's herb garden). while you were away the smith climbed to hearthold to deliver a gate hinge. ashe rang the bell as she came through the herb garden. the smith set her hammer on the wall, and stood a moment.

> **Ashe**: she heard it. she put the hammer down. then picked it up. a good start, by the bell.  
> **smith**: the lavender's grown. when did the lavender grow.

**3. the bell at the forge** (Cinderwick's sand). while you were away ashe carried the bedtime bell down to the forge itself and rang it at sunset. the smith banked the fire, walked to cinderwick, and dozed on the warm black sand till morning.

**Spar (optional):** the smith: "anvil's resting. my arms aren't. a friendly bout by the lava? the bell says when."

**For the map lane:** the Ember forge with its door half shut and the glow dimmed; the forge banked: door shut, low ember glow, hammer on a hook; the smith asleep on Cinderwick's black sand.

## Lumen: the gap in the chart

**The stargazer**, and Mote who joins; ring rival: the stargazer. The stargazer counts every star on a chart with one gap and never closes her eyes; the gap was never a star up there but a small one down here, and it closes them for her.

**1. one short** (the habitat bunk). while you were away the stargazer counted the stars over the dream grove and came up one short again. a tiny light in a nightcap drifted round her, humming.

> **stargazer**: four thousand and twelve... and then this gap. there was a star here.  
> **Mote**: mm. hello. she's counting. she's always counting. it's very busy up there.  
> **companion**: there's a little star with a hat on. it's floating on my leaf.

**2. stars in the pool** (the dream pools). while you were away the stargazer counted the stars reflected in the dream pools, to check her sums. mote floated down and sat on the water, right where the gap would be.

> **Mote**: mm. i'm the gap. i came down for a nap and never went back up.  
> **stargazer**: you're in my pool. you're the right size. you're in a nightcap.  
> **companion**: she didn't count anything for ages. she just looked.

_Stays changed:_ the stargazer's chart is pinned up in the habitat with a tiny nightcap drawn in the gap.

**3. lights out** (the hammock under the moon tree). while you were away mote settled on the stargazer's eyelids like a warm crumb of dust. she lay in the hammock under the moon tree and, for the first time, let the stars count themselves.

> **Mote**: mm. eyes shut. the stars are still there. they don't need watching.  
> **stargazer**: they're all accounted for. one's just... down here. that's fine.  
> **companion**: she's asleep. the moon tree is glowing very softly so it won't wake her.

_Stays changed:_ the stargazer sleeps in the hammock under the moon tree with her chart rolled up beside it, and the last crystal spire round the Dream Grove glows.

**Mote asks:** "mm. the moon's nearly full. i'm the bit that's left. shall we finish it together?" Mote floats up off the stargazer's pillow, tips its nightcap to the moon tree, and floats a step behind you, beside your grassling: the moon is full.

**Spar (optional):** the stargazer: "a bout in the sparring crater? gravity's low, so we'll mostly float about politely."

**For the map lane:** the star chart pinned up in the habitat with a nightcap doodle in its gap; the stargazer asleep in the hammock, chart rolled beside it; the fourteenth crystal spire lit.
