# What makes MyGame a good game (Matt, 2 Nov: "really think hard about what makes this a good game")

Every task is judged against these pillars. A beautiful world that isn't fun to play is a failure, so play comes first.

## The five pillars

1. **It feels good in the first 30 seconds.** Moving, swinging, shooting and dodging must be responsive and juicy: hit-stop, knockback, enemy tells, a dodge with clear timing, a crisp sound on every hit. Each of the 14 folk plays differently, not just with different numbers. This is Zelda's lesson, and Eastward's weak spot.
2. **Every screen gives you a reason to look around.** Density beats size. Each screen (40 x 22.5 cells) holds something to find, use or notice: a secret, a resident doing something, a shortcut, a view of a landmark. The world only grows when the new ground can be filled to that standard.
3. **Coming home shows what you did.** Crossroads is the emotional heart. Every shard, quest and Glint spent visibly changes it: fences mended, the well running, stalls reopened, folk moving in, raids repelled. The player should want to walk back through the village after every outing.
4. **Each region plays differently.** Every region brings its own verb or pressure, not just a new tileset:
   - the Frostline: cold and shelter;
   - Sunstone: water and heat;
   - the fire country: lava timing;
   - Cogsworth: rail and machines;
   - Neon Harbour: verticality, crowds and the Company's grip;
   - the Isles: ferries and tides;
   - Highgarth: the closed pass and stealth past the keep.

   Each has one dungeon with its own puzzle idea.

5. **The theme is played, not told.** The circle shares the moon's light, and the Company sells it. The player sees the Company take things (the timber camp, fenced yards, the bought shard light) and restores them for everyone. Folk, quests and rewards all tie back to the circle.

## Respect the player

- Clear goals, fast travel between waystones, no grind, and short sessions that still move something forward.
- Losing costs a little Glint, never progress.
- Touch and pad controls are first-class. The game reads well at 1080p and on a phone.

## Tests every PR answers (one line each in the PR body)

- **Feel:** does this make moment-to-moment play better, or is it neutral?
- **Discovery:** for a world change, what does each new screen offer?
- **Home:** for progression, how does Crossroads show it?
- **Region:** does this strengthen the region's own verb?
- **Theme:** does it fit the circle-and-Company story?

## What this changes now

- **Play before polish.** Matt plays a build every week from the first playable hour. Feel and fun issues outrank art polish.
- **A combat feel pass** (task F1) runs straight after the slice: hit-stop, knockback, enemy tells, dodge timing and sound on the existing cast and enemies, tested by playing.
- **Discovery density** joins the town and zone checks: at least one point of interest per screen, listed in each plan.
- **Village change states:** Crossroads gets visible restoration stages wired to quest flags (task H1).
