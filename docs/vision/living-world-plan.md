# The living world: merged plan and schedule

Approved by Matt on 17 Oct 2026 (19:16): "try to implement most of the ideas … schedule everything in a way that makes sense". This merges his 11:54 brief (currency and decay, menus, biomes, decorating, interiors, NPCs, visual depth) with Codex's proposals (seasons, weather and time as a scenery layer; a world with a memory; the destination map). `coherence-plan.md` still holds the art rules and the Codex lanes; this file holds the order of the new work.

## Rules that hold for every phase

- **Screen time first.** The game rewards time away from the phone. Nothing asks the player to come back, watch an animation or keep a streak alive to avoid loss.
- **The currency (GRASS) is never lost, stolen or expired.** It only goes down when the player spends it.
- **Decay is visual, capped and reversible.** It never deletes anything the player owns, and it only reverses through a repair the player chooses.
- **Atmosphere is presentation.** Season, weather and time of day change props, light and activities, never rewards, mood, timers or progress.
- **Lasting history is saved and never decays.** A built shelter, a chosen library edition or a befriended resident stays. A long absence gives one readable welcome, not a backlog.
- **Same art rules everywhere** (`coherence-plan.md`, `art-pipeline.md`): the shared grid, the steep top-down view for buildings, material detail, silhouette shadows, night light. New biomes are drawn with the same toolkit from the first prop.
- **Phones first.** Every new layer has an image-memory cost in `check:images`; reduced motion shows a still; touch targets stay at least 44 px.

## Who does what

Claude draws, lays out and wires the screens. Codex builds the rules, stores, save migrations and tests underneath, each as a small PR (lanes in `coherence-plan.md`). A phase starts when its Codex engine exists or alongside it; Claude never builds UI against an API that is not on main.

## The open world (approved by Matt, 17 Oct 19:47)

Matt: "create a sort of small open world … with small biomes where you reach the next when you go to the right, left, up or down … and your main hub with your house that you can decorate". Decisions:

- **A small region of screen-sized zones.** Walk off an edge and keep walking: the next zone is already there and the camera scrolls across in one piece (`world-atlas.md`, "Seamless travel"). Each zone is one generated map at the garden's size with its own terrain family and exits on matching edges. The world stays small: a handful of zones, not a continent.
- **The hub is the garden and the house**, with a house interior to decorate (was L7).
- **Crossroads Town lies through the garden gate, south of the garden,** with Moss's stall (the Shop), the library and the sparring ring. The missions map becomes the region map: the same selection, START and guards, plus travel to zones already visited.
- **Exploring costs nothing extra.** Matt: "there should not be a cost to exploring beyond the general rules established before … the only cost is being on your phone in general, especially during sleep hours". No steps, stamina or daily visits; world health (L3) is the only pressure.
- **Missions are unchanged:** the companion travels while the player is away. Walking to a place is optional scenery and residents, never a shortcut to rewards.
- **Gates:** Matt approved world unlocks on 20 Oct (01:05 and 01:25): the world opens progressively, way by way, through rest tasks or GRASS, at most one way a day, never closing again ([world-unlocks.md](world-unlocks.md)). The mountain outing's level 2 gate stays as it is.
- **Pokémon-like pieces, in NapAttack's terms:** residents to befriend in each zone (the visitor system), one sparring ring per biome with a resident rival and its own backdrop, keepsakes from each zone as decor at home, the region map with fast travel.

**The region, laid out so it makes sense** (height runs north, water runs south and east, and every exit matches the garden as it is drawn today):

                 [Snowy Ridge]            north: up the trail through the garden's treeline (the mountain's level 2 gate)

 [Woodland] ---- [Garden + house] ---- [Riverside]   west and east: along the garden's stream, which flows east

The existing mission worlds become zones: the library street joins Crossroads Town, the fountain park becomes the Town Park, and the mountain world becomes the Snowy Ridge. The worlds already have a ground, objects, a walk grid and a following camera (`scripts/art/worlds.py`, `components/world-scene.tsx`), so the zone engine extends them rather than starting again. The current missions map, which shows the land beyond the gate with the mountain at its top, is a stop-gap: in W2 it becomes the region map above, with the ridge north of the garden instead of beyond the gate.

## World logic (Matt, 17 Oct: "make sure the logic is right")

Every map has to make sense as a place before it looks good. These hold for the garden, the land beyond the gate, the mission worlds and every new biome:

- **Height runs up the map.** Higher ground sits toward the top (north) and is reached by steps, a ramp or a switchback road, never by walking off a meadow onto it. A drop is drawn as a stone or earth face with a lit lip and its shadow on the ground below.
- **Climate follows height and water.** Snow lies only on high ground (above a face or on peaks), sand only beside water or in a desert biome, reeds and mud only at water's edge. A building belongs to its ground: the cabin on the mountain terrace, the stall at the crossroads, the library by the road into town.
- **Water runs downhill** and roads cross it by bridges or stepping stones.
- **Every place is reachable on foot** from the entrance, and every road leads somewhere.
- **Generators export the terrain** (`missionMapTerrain` in `lib/mission-map.ts`: one letter per 4 x 4 cell) so these rules are checked by scripts, not only by eye. `check:mission-departure` already checks the land beyond the gate (reachability, steps as the only way up, snow only above the face); C15 generalises it.

## Varied terrain, customisation and battles (proposed 17 Oct, after Matt's ask)

Matt asked for more varied terrain and for the customisation and battles that come with it. The terrain and art below are approved direction; anything that touches rewards or battle rules is marked **proposal (waiting for Matt)** and is built only after he says so.

**Terrain families**, each a set of ground, edge and prop rules in the toolkit, reused by every place that has that ground:

**Customisation that comes with terrain:**

- Keepsakes from each place become decor in the garden (a cairn stone from the ridge, a lantern from the library lane, shells from the cove), placed with the C12 decorating model. Proposal (waiting for Matt): which missions grant which keepsake.
- Garden ground styles: paths in sand, gravel or stepping stones; fence styles (picket, wattle, stone wall); hut paint and roof tiles. Presentation only, bought with GRASS at prices Matt sets.
- Companion outfits by terrain (a scarf for the ridge, wellies for the riverside, a sun hat for the oasis) in the existing hat system.

**Battles that come with terrain:**

- The battle backdrop and platform follow where the fight happens: the meadow ring, a snowy ridge clearing, a riverside jetty, an oasis courtyard. Presentation only, drawn with the battle layers (sky, far, near, platform).
- **Approved (Matt, 17 Oct 19:47):** one gentle terrain condition per field that changes how a card plays (snow slows the first move, rain soaks fire-flavoured cards, sand hides one card), balanced against the existing battle simulator (C17) before anything ships; Matt sees the numbers first. No condition may make raids harsher or cost GRASS.
- Proposal (waiting for Matt): visiting rivals bring their home terrain to raids, so a raid from the ridge arrives in the snow.

Characters (coherence step 8) are already drawn with sphere shading and outlines; they get a readability check against the new world rather than a redraw, and the welcome-back scene's companion size is matched to the scene's pixel size during L1.

## How Codex keeps ideating

Codex reads this file and `coherence-plan.md` on every run. When its queue is empty it play-tests the latest main and writes ideas in `docs/codex/ideas/<topic>.md`, each grounded in what is on main now (the steep top-down buildings, the detail toolkit, night light, this schedule) and in the rules above: problem, proposal, which phase it extends, the art it needs, risks, effort and open questions for Matt. Claude reviews new idea files on each pass, folds the good ones into a phase here and answers under Feedback in `coherence-plan.md`. Nothing in an idea file is built until it appears in this schedule.
