# Cliff core: one rock system for every cliff

Status: built by Claude A on 21 Oct from the cliff bake-off, as a draft for Matt; a polish round followed the same day (stairs cut from the crag, drifted snow, a calmer lawn, falls scaled to their drop, steep rims stepped). Nothing in the game uses it yet: no zone is wired to it. Code: `scripts/art/cliff_core.py`. Pictures: `docs/vision/cliff-core/` (written by the generator, reproduced by `npm run check:art-repro`).

## Why

Three rounds of the mountain kit (PR #148) still read as grey masonry columns beside Sea of Stars, and Matt rejected the patched summit ("non of this looks natural (given the soft grid), there is no verticality, hard edges"). A bake-off of three cliff swatches (organic rock masses, layered strata, autotiles) was graded twice, by eye and by method. The look grader chose the rock masses; the method grader chose a hybrid that can paint a continent. This is that hybrid, built exactly to the graders' plan:

1. **Core.** The layered-strata entrant's absolute-height bands as the structure, on the terrace toolkit's soft grid, with the autotile entrant's world-anchored hash noise.
2. **Look.** The rock-mass entrant's mass and plate shading inside the bands, with the look grader's five fixes.
3. **Kit.** Dressing placed by rule, four `STRATA` tables with a one-screen demo each, and the outputs registered with art-repro.

## How it works

**Input: a world height field.** `World(Z, table)` takes a plan height field (`Z[py, x]`, the height in px of the ground standing at plan row `py`; screen row = `py - top - Z`), plus optional plan masks for paths, water and forced snow, and a list of `Stair`s. Heights are tiers on the soft grid (32 px, 40 in the city and the mesa). `from_rims()` builds a field from rims drawn as screen curves; `random_world()` builds one from noise. `walk_tiers(world)` returns the screen tier map `terrace.build()` takes, so a zone painted with the core walks on the same map.

**Rims are contours, not columns.** For each tier level the boundary of `Z >= level` is traced as closed crack-following loops (pixel edges, inside on the left), smoothed and measured by arc length. So an outline may lobe, bay, spur and curl back on itself. Every face pixel carries its loop, its arc along the rim, its depth below the rim and its absolute height `z`. The tracing is global and deterministic.

**Steep rims are stepped.** Where a rim runs more than 1.3 rows a column, its face would be a thin slanted strip, so the field is held level across each 16 px (two soft-grid cells) of that run, at the row the rim has in the middle, and the corners are rounded: the run becomes short full-height faces with side bands between them. Lowest tier first; a higher tier is never cut.

**The painter.** Plan rows are walked north to south (nearer ground drawn over farther): each plan pixel draws its top at `py - top - z`, and where its southern neighbour is lower a face drops to it. Faces exist on south edges only; where an edge runs north to south the top shows a 5 px side band (lit facing west, dark facing east), as in `terrace.py`.

**Strata in absolute height.** A table's bands are `(thickness, family, texture)` from `z = 0` upward, repeating. A band at height `z` shows at `rim row + (rim z - z)`, so one band runs through every face of a region whatever its outline, and the ledges sit on band tops. A band's family biases its plates among the rock's siblings (`crag`, `crag_warm`, `crag_cool`), so bands read as subtle layering inside the masses rather than a layer cake.

**Masses inside the bands.** Along each rim the arc is cut into crag forms 18 to 46 px wide (the table's `mass_w`), each break wandering 1.5 px with height so no fissure is ruled. A 64 px face splits into an upper and a lower course about seven times in ten, at a height that varies per crag, and the lower course has its own breaks, so fissures never line up course to course; a 32 px face splits one time in ten. Each crag:

- stands upright: its bands and plates are level, measured from the rim at its middle, so on a sloping rim crags step instead of shearing (no diagonal stripes);
- is shaded as one rounded lump lit from the upper left (the bake-off's `shade_mass`): a dome from the distance to its outline, a normal from the dome's slope, four tones with one hard step each, a glint at the top left, the underside dark;
- has rounded shoulders (a radius up to 9 px) and sometimes sits a few px under the rim, so its top line lobes; the notch between shoulders is a deep recess;
- meets its neighbour in a vertical fissure (tone 0 of the cool sibling) that runs down from the rim only part of the way in about half the crags; below that the two are one rock, with no line between them;
- carries a second vertical fissure when it is wider than 30 px (the Sea of Stars columns), lit on its east wall;
- is split into rounded plates on a jittered lattice in (arc, height): each plate one sibling, its top and left edges a tone up, its bottom and right edges a tone down (tone 0 deep in shade), the odd plate set back; band partings and the bands' textures (laminae, pebbles, flutes) show inside;
- the odd crag is set back a tone (deep zones), the foot is damp (a tone down).

**World-anchored noise.** Every choice is a 32-bit integer hash (`hashu`, numpy-vectorised, integer arithmetic only) of world coordinates or of a loop and its arc and height. A window is painted with a 48 px margin and cropped, and every rule reaches less than that, so any window of the world renders the same pixels. The check renders a 512 px world whole and as four 256 px tiles: every tile equals its crop of the whole, and the seam between two adjacent tiles differs by 0 px.

`STRATA` holds one table a setting; a table is a region's geology and its dressing densities. Each has a one-screen demo (320 x 384, a phone screen at the game's scale) to copy:

| `canyon`   | 40   | `clay`, `adobe` and `bleached` bands                                                                                            | sand with adobe patches and grit | `demo_canyon`: a mesa top, a bench and the floor, a dry wash, a stair to the bench                                                                                                          |
| `city`     | 40   | dressed `ashlar` courses (a built face: blocks 8 to 15 long in their own bond per course, weathered, moss in the lowest joints) | setts in world coordinates       | `demo_city`: a retaining wall with a coping between two streets, a stair with cheeks                                                                                                        |

How a lane uses it: copy a demo function, draw the zone's rims (or its plan field), pass the zone's paths, water and stairs, pick the table and call `render(world)` for the ground picture and `walk_tiers(world)` for the walk map. A new setting is a new table. Never patch a picture: change the field or the table and regenerate.

The rock passes both cliff kinds at once in every box, which pull opposite ways (stone-wall wants larger, calmer clusters; face wants more shapes). Margins are thin: stone-wall cluster 3.29 against a floor of 3.27 in outline 1 (its box holds a stepped corner); ground tones 10.8 to 11.3 against 10.7 (85 %), short of the 11.3 that 90 % would ask, and shapes 28.9 against 28.7. Lone pixels are 0.9 to 1.3 % of each picture (the ledger allows 6 %), every pixel is on the house palette. The whole-picture west-minus-east of the lint is about zero (−2.5 to −0.9): on a scene it measures where the cliffs happen to lie, so the check measures it the way the lint measures a sprite, on every crag as its own sprite.

The other demos: coast rock and ground pass; the canyon's rock and sand pass; the city wall passes as `stone-wall` and `face` (tones 16.5), its setts pass `ground`.

**Compared by eye** at 3x, everything at an adult of 25 px, beside the Sea of Stars ledge path (press kit, Traversal-4), the Sea of Stars cliff top over the void, and Eastward's Octopia rock cliff and its grass ledges with a stair. The comparison picture is built with `python3 scripts/art/cliff_core.py --compare REFDIR OUT.png` from local copies of the official screenshots; it is not committed, because the screenshots stay out of the repository (as for the detail bar's baselines).

## Honest weaknesses

- **Still lighter and greener than Sea of Stars.** The rock now reads as warm rounded crags, but Sea of Stars' faces are more columnar at a finer scale, its grass is a dark olive and its scenes are packed with plants; ours keeps the house palette's light lawn (now calmer: clusters and the cool sibling) and the brief's wide meadow, so a screen is mostly lawn.
- **Stairs** now read as stone blocks with mossy cheeks, but a long flight is still a regular rhythm of tread and riser, closer to dressed steps than to a path worn into rock.
- **Snow** drifts are outlined by their shade bands (3 px), which can read as drawn contours at 3x; the smallest rocks are only a few px; the cornice is short where the rim is short.
- **Steep diagonal rims** (more than 1.3 rows a column, about 52 degrees) are now capped as short stepped faces, 16 px a step (`World._step_steep`), with rounded corners; the steps are square blocks at 3x, and the box checked for the detail bar can fall on one (outline 1).
- **The fall** is wider by rule now, but most of its plunge pool lies under the face's foot, so little open water shows; the mist is sparse; there is no animation (the falls are not yet in `water_flow.py`).
- **Ground bar margins are thin** (tones at 86 to 89 % of Eastward's lawn in three of four boxes); the calmer lawn passes on the house's 85 % ruling for ramp-built art, not on the code's 90 %.
- **New palette family.** `crag` is a new accent (`accent:highland`), added to `BASES` and inserted into `art/house-palette.json` without rewriting the rest of the file. It is awaiting Matt: he decides on accents.
- **Not wired in.** No zone uses the core; the world canvas, the asset ledger and the walk map have not seen it. Not checked under the night tint, on a phone, or in the game.
- **Tracing is in Python.** A 512 x 705 plan field traces in 0.06 s and renders in 1.7 s on one core; a whole continent (thousands of px a side) has not been timed.
