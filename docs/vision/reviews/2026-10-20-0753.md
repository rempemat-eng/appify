## Comparison: Cogsworth against Stardew Valley's Railroad and Eastward's towns, and the Frostline against Link's Awakening's Tal Tal range

The earlier runs compared the valley coast, the isles and Neon, and Claude D compared Sunstone. This run takes Cogsworth, which no review has compared yet, and the Frostline's edge with the valley. I have not played these games for this note. Claims stay at what the sources say.

### Stardew Valley's Railroad (official Stardew Valley wiki)

From the official wiki's [Railroad](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Railroad) and [Train](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Train) pages:

- The Railroad lies north of the Mountain and opens only after an earthquake clears the pass. The way in is a named event, not a gap in the map.
- Trains pass through on random days. The page names a "right tunnel": the tracks enter and leave through tunnels, so the line has somewhere to come from and somewhere to go.
- The Train Platform sits "north of the tracks". Each of the area's other features has a use: the Spa's pool restores energy, and a cave north-east of the platform leads to the Witch's Swamp.

What reads better there, and what NapAttack should take:

- **A railway never runs into nothing.** Tunnels at the screen's sides make the edge of the map part of the world. Do the same for Cogsworth's main line (finding 1): a tunnel portal in a brick cutting at x 0 and at x 320 of the Junction. Do the same for any later line that leaves a region.
- **Every feature has a stated use.** Cogsworth's ring, mast, crane and "lock" (finding 6) each need the one use a player can read: a path to the ring, a stair up the mast, a jib over a boat, a lock with a step in the water.

### Eastward's lived-in towns (Pixpil)

Pixpil, in [Game Developer's "Road to the IGF" interview (Joel Couture, 19 Mar 2020)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-pixpil-s-i-eastward-i-):

- Lead artist Hong Moran: "we wanted to make it look lively because people are still living there".
- Moran: "I hope people can find at least one special part of each character and environment."
- Producer Zhou: "Strong colors, various buildings and objects, rich and subtle details", so as to "invite them to explore and connect with the world."

What reads better there: open ground between buildings is used. Cogsworth's plaza is dense round the clock tower, but the Junction's whole south half (y 420 to 576) and the Canal's lawns either side of the street are open grass in an industrial town.

What NapAttack should change (Claude C, decorative only):

1. Turn the Junction's south lawn into a goods yard:
   - a coal stage and a stack of sleepers against a brick wall;
   - two wagons on a short siding that ends at buffers by the platform's east end, which also gives the ring's turntable look a reason, or removes it;
   - the ring set in a corner of the yard on trodden earth, with its own path.
2. Give the canal's two towpaths their own clutter, against the walls and not in the walking band:
   - mooring rings and a coiled rope at the narrowboat;
   - a lock-keeper's hut at the lock;
   - sacks on the quay under the crane.
3. Give each of the Canal zone's four buildings one special part, as Moran asks of every environment:
   - the warehouse's hoist door open, with a sack on the hook;
   - the cottage's window box;
   - the factory's coal barrows at the furnace;
   - a mechanic at the hangar's door.

### Link's Awakening's Tal Tal range (Zelda Wiki)

From [Zelda Wiki's Tal Tal Mountain Range page](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Tal_Tal_Mountain_Range) (a community wiki):

- The range is "the northern-most part of the map", "the source of most of the island's water", with several waterfalls from its eastern part.
- It is reached directly only from Tal Tal Heights to its south, plus a small part from the swamp to the south-west.

What reads better there: the mountains are one barrier with few named ways up, and the water visibly comes down out of them.

NapAttack's Frostline already has the second half: the lake, the fall over the escarpment, the Woodland's spring. Its weak point is the first half. The barrier changes height and style on the zone lines:

- the Snowmelt's low scarp against the Saddle's tall face (finding 3);
- the escarpment's foot on the Woodland seam (finding 4);
- the snow switching texture at x 320 (finding 8).

What NapAttack should change (Claude A, with Claude E for the Ridge):

1. Draw the Frostline's step as one contour line across the Snowmelt, the Saddle and the Ridge, at one height, with the trail, the switchback and the Ridge's stair as its only ways up. Each way up should be marked by a cairn or a signpost, as the Ridge's stair already is.
2. Let the meltwater show where it leaves the high ground: a white fall with a pool at each tier, one per step, so the player reads the mountains as the valley's source from below.
