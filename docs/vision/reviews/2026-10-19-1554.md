## Findings, most severe first

### 1. Neon's market stalls and tram stop stand across the carriageway

- **Where:**
  - Market, neon (0, -1): the four stalls on Market Street (`stall-27` to `stall-30`), x 20 to 300, y 285 to 326.
  - Lantern Plaza, neon (1, -2): the tram stop (`city_tram_stop`, foot (300, 335)), x 270 to 320, y 290 to 335.
- **Owner:** Claude C (`city_zones.py`, `city_props_placed`).

Both are placed with their feet on the south pavement, as the code intends. But the pavement is about 8 px deep and each sprite is about 35 to 45 px tall, so in the three-quarter view each sprite's body covers the whole road behind it. The lane markings run in under the stalls. In the browser and in the stitched view, Market Street reads as blocked by four stalls standing in the traffic. Hill Street reads as ending in a bus shelter at the plaza's east side. There are no tram rails anywhere for the stop to serve.

This breaks world-rules 2a ("nothing heavy blocks a path") as the player sees it, even though the walk grid is right.

**Action:**

- Make Market Street a market street: drop its lane markings and paint it as setts from the avenue to the old core, with bollards at both ends. The stalls then stand on a pedestrian street, like the night market they are meant to be.
- Alternatively, widen the south pavement to at least the stalls' depth (about 40 px) and set them back on it.
- For the tram stop, either lay tram rails along Hill Street (in from the waterfront, ending at a buffer by the stop), or move the shelter onto the plaza's paving, facing the road, with its back to the fountain.

### 2. The Heights' avenue is a road with a centre line that climbs two stairs and ends in a loop

- **Where:** The Heights, neon (0, -2), x 115 to 180, y 0 to 260. The stairs are at y 145 to 170 and y 228 to 252. The loop at the top is at y 0 to 45 (stand `lookout` at (150, 60)).
- **Owner:** Claude C.

The avenue keeps its carriageway and its dashed yellow centre line all the way up the hill. Flights of steps are drawn across it twice, so it cannot be a road for vehicles, yet it is painted as one. At the top it turns in a hairpin loop walled in on three sides, with nothing at the lookout to look from.

World-rules 2a: "a footpath never turns into a road for no reason", and paths widen where people stop.

**Action:**

- Above Hill Street, draw the avenue as a stepped pedestrian street: setts, no lane markings, handrails at the flights, lamps on its terrace walls (already there).
- Replace the loop with a small paved lookout terrace: a railing on its south edge, one bench facing south over the city, and a coin telescope.

### 3. Beacon Point's quay road ends in a blob, and the lighthouse causeway is a ghost

- **Where:** Beacon Point, neon (2, 0).
  - The quay road: x 95 to 125, y 0 to 275.
  - The causeway: x 160 to 260, y 400 to 490.
- **Owner:** Claude C.

The quay road coming down from the Docks has wobbling edges, unlike every other street in the city, and no kerbs. It ends in a rounded blob in the middle of the pedestrian paving. A street ends at a junction, a turning circle or a gate, never in a square (world-rules 3).

The breakwater out to the lighthouse is a half-transparent checker band over the water. It has no top, no sides and no wet foot, so it reads as a shadow rather than a wall you could walk along (world-rules 2: `supported` needs drawn supports).

**Action:**

- Draw the breakwater as a stone mole: a lit coping on its upper-left edge, a dark wet face in shadow on the lower right, a foam line where it meets the sea, and a short stair down from the quay.

## Comparison: Neon Harbour and the Pokémon Black and White Castelia City, with Eastward

The 08:04 run compared the valley coast and the isles. Claude D compared Sunstone. This run takes the new nine-zone city, the region Matt asked to be "a bigger bustling city with lots and lots of high buildings and possibly verticality". I have not played these games for this note. Claims stay at what the sources say.

### Castelia City (Pokémon Black and White)

From Bulbapedia's page on [Castelia City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Castelia) (a community wiki, not an official one; used here only for the city's layout):

- The skyscrapers stand in a central business district, reached by narrow alleyways lined with small shops.
- "Central Plaza" is where all the main streets meet, a park with a large fountain.
- The streets each have one character:
  - Castelia Street: a busy commuter street of tall buildings;
  - Mode Street: lined with trees and plants;
  - Narrow Street: a dim back alley with dumpsters;
  - Gym Street.
- Oceanfront Road, the wharf street, runs round the outside as the city's outer layer.
- Five piers, "shaped like fingers", each lie opposite one of the streets: Liberty Pier opposite Gym Street, Prime Pier across from Mode Street, the Cruise Dock opposite Castelia Street.

Game Freak's design notes, quoted by [Siliconera (Ishaan Sahdev, 21 Aug 2011)](https://siliconera.com/excerpts-from-pokemon-black-whites-castelia-city-design-documents/):

- The port "is shaped like hand"; "Open hand is the image of C3".
- Each finger is assigned a use: a patrol vessel on odd-numbered days, a shuttle cruiser from the ring finger.
- "Since it is pier, it is floating on the sea. Surface is thin concrete. Low fence is placed other than platform."

What reads better there:

- **The street is the unit, and each has one job and one look.** You know which street you are on from its trees, its shopfronts or its gloom, not from a sign. Neon's streets (Hill Street, Market Street, Tower Row, the quay road) are all the same asphalt with the same yellow dashes and the same lamps. Only the props beside them differ.
- **One plaza where the streets meet, and the city's edge made of the sea.** The wharf road wraps the district. Each pier sits opposite a street, so walking down a street ends at the water and a boat.
- **Every pier has a stated use and a drawn edge** (thin concrete, a low fence), so nothing on the water is vague.

What NapAttack should change in Neon Harbour (Claude C):

1. Give each street one character, and change the road surface to match it:
   - Market Street a pedestrian market of setts and canopies (finding 1);
   - Tower Row a wide boulevard with trees in grates and wider pavements;
   - the Heights' avenue a stepped street (finding 2);
   - the old core's lanes narrow and dim, with dumpsters and back doors.

   Keep lane markings for Hill Street and the quay road only.

2. Line up each street's south or east end with something on the water:
   - Tower Row runs down to the Skyport concourse;
   - Hill Street runs to the waterfront's water-taxi steps;
   - the quay road runs to the lighthouse mole (finding 3).

   That makes the end of each walk a view.

3. Give the docks' cranes and the water taxis one drawn edge each: a low fence or bollards along the apron's sea edge, and a ladder or steps at each taxi berth. That way nothing meets the water without a kerb.

### Eastward's lived-in towns

Pixpil, in [Game Developer's "Road to the IGF" interview (Joel Couture, 19 Mar 2020)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-pixpil-s-i-eastward-i-):

- Lead artist Hong Moran: "we wanted to make it look lively because people are still living there, actively, in their own unique way."
- Moran: "I hope people can find at least one special part of each character and environment."

What reads better there: lots are never just ground. Where Neon still has open asphalt between towers (in the browser, the Plaza's lots either side of its south road, y 340 to 430, above the buildings that rise from Downtown and the Market), Eastward's towns put the residents' own clutter: drying laundry, potted plants, a bike, crates by a back door.

What NapAttack should change (Claude C, decorative only, AGENTS.md: no new rules):

1. Turn each empty lot into one small place:
   - a pocket park with two trees and a bench behind the Plaza's east block;
   - a car park with three parked cars and a ticket machine in the Waterfront's lower lot;
   - a basketball court or a rooftop-garden plot in the Heights.
2. Give each tower base one "special part":
   - a noodle bar's steam;
   - a cat on a step;
   - a flickering sign that is the only one in its street.

   That way no two blocks share a frontage.
