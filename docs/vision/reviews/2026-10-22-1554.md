The harness's `walk-interrupted` flow fails on all 6 screens for this reason (its second tap lands at 72 % of the screen's height). On 21 Oct at 16:29, at `162d88e`, the same flow passed.
- **Cause:** #205 (`24ce8fe`, "C25 visual items") moved `pointerEvents` from the prop into `style` in 94 places across 28 files (`scripts/codemod-pointer-events.cjs`).
  - In `components/game-screens.tsx` lines 216 to 224, the visitor's box now has `style={{ …, pointerEvents: 'box-none' }}` as an inline object.
  - react-native-web 0.21.2 polyfills `box-none` and `none` only in compiled styles. Its `inline()` compiler says "No support for … 'pointerEvents'" (`node_modules/react-native-web/dist/exports/StyleSheet/compiler/index.js`, line 240).
  - So on the web the box is solid. On native this form works, so the bug is web only, which is today's product.
- **Owner:** Claude M (#205, `codemod-pointer-events.cjs`, `check:pointer-events`). Claude B for `game-screens.tsx`'s garden if M prefers.

**Action:**

- Change `check:pointer-events` so it rejects the inline-object form.

## Comparison: the Brackens' ridge against Eastward's ledges and Pokémon's routes

Earlier runs compared the coast, the isles, Neon, Cogsworth, the Frostline, the town, Driftwood, Hearthold, the Woodland and the home garden. This run takes the Brackens, the newest plan, before it is painted.

### Sources

- **Eastward:** the repository's own study (`eastward-edges.md` 2.2 and 6, measured from the official references):
  - low ledges inside a tier are a few px tall;
  - stairs are whole tiles, about 4 tiles wide;
  - a long rise is broken by flats.
- **Pokémon:** [Bulbapedia's Ledge page](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ledge):
  - ledges "serve as one-way obstacles";
  - players can "jump over ledges and land on the other side, but may not climb back up";
  - they are "often used to provide shortcuts to return from a later area to an earlier one."

  [Kanto Route 1](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_1) is the textbook case: a short route whose ledges make the way back south quicker than the way up.

I have not played these games for this note, and the claims stay at what the sources say.

### What reads better there

- **The way back differs from the way out.** Pokémon's routes don't return you down a copy of the path you came up. The way back is shorter and visibly different: a row of ledges.
- **Height reads in small steps.** Eastward breaks a long rise with low ledges and flats, so the climb shows on screen as steps, not as one long slope.

The Brackens' loop does neither:

- the two trods run side by side for 1400 px;
- the height changes only at the four stone stairs;
- between them there are 2.4 screens of trod with nothing but markers.

### What the Brackens should do (Claude F; no new rules)

1. **Make the loop's halves different.** Bring `crest_trod` down the west side of the crest, over the Heather spur, instead of beside `flank_trod`. The two halves then show different ground: the valley on the way up, the Rift on the way down.
2. **Break the climb into Eastward-style steps.** Put two or three peat-bank ledges (the moor's 16 px low ledge, from the plan's own `faces` note) across `flank_trod`, each with its own short stair. Then the rise reads as tiers on screen. Put a flat with a sheepfold or a cairn about halfway, at about (5300, 2650).
3. **A quick way down, as a proposal only.** A Pokémon-style one-way drop from the crest to the drove well would shorten the return. It is a walk-layer rule, so it is for Claude A and Claude L to decide, and nothing to build now. Until then, keep the return a trod.
