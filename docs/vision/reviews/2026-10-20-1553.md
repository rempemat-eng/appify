## Findings, most severe first

### 1. Desert dunes run straight into Hearthold's ash and lava moat

- **Where:** Sunstone Reach, the scenery cell west of Hearthold, west (0, -1), against Hearthold (1, -1) at x 0, y 0 to 576 (region px x 640, y 576 to 1152).
- **Owner:** Claude A (`scenery.py`, the west region's cells).

In the browser, from Hearthold's courtyard, the land west of the moat is open dunes in the Gulch's sand colours. They meet Hearthold's grey ash on a dead-straight vertical line at the zone edge, and the lava moat runs about 30 px from that line. Hatched basalt shapes sit on top of the dunes without blending in.

This breaks three rules:

- world-rules 4 (climate grades): sand goes to scrub and grass, and lava goes to crust and ash. Sand never meets lava country directly.
- world-rules 4: the ground tone matches in the shared strip.
- art-faults rule 13: world borders are drawn as real scenery.

**Action:** paint west (0, -1), and the west of (0, -2) beside the Caldera, as the fire country. Basalt ridges and ash should come first. Let the ash grade into cooled crust, then scrub, then sand only towards the Gulch's row (y 1152 and below). Carry the Hearthold zone's own ash tone and dressing across x 0.

### 2. The Summit's mountain is a pattern, not a landform

- **Where:** the Summit, valley (0, -3), x 0 to 320, y 0 to 440, and its flanks down to y 576.
- **Owner:** Claude A (`frost_zones.summit`).

The rock is drawn as rounded grey blobs that drip downward through vertically hatched snow, like melted wax. There is no lit top, no shadowed side and no ridgeline. Nothing says which way the ground falls.

The viewing platform sits in an oval of snow halfway up this pattern. In the browser, the scenery mountains at the left edge of the same view are drawn properly, as massifs in the three-quarter view with lit west faces. The Summit, which is the valley's highest landmark, is drawn worse than the backdrop behind it.

This misses the art-style bar ("Eastward's level of detail is required"). It also breaks rule 2 (light from the upper left, materials in their ramp) and world-rules 2a (height changes only at drawn faces).

**Action:** draw the Summit as one massif with the same tools as `scenery.py`'s mountains:

- ridgelines and a peak above the platform;
- lit west faces and shadowed east faces;
- gullies with snow lying in them, and snow caps only on surfaces that face up;
- the platform as a ledge cut into the peak's south shoulder, with a lit lip and a retaining wall;
- the trail from the Saddle climbing to it in two switchbacks.

## Comparison: Crossroads Town and Driftwood against Mabe Village and Pelican Town, and Hearthold against A Link to the Past's Hyrule Castle

Earlier runs compared the valley coast, the isles, Neon, Cogsworth and the Frostline, and Claude D compared Sunstone's desert and caldera. This run takes the valley's town, which no review has compared yet, and Hearthold. I have not played these games for this note. Claims stay at what the sources say.

### Mabe Village (Link's Awakening) and Pelican Town (Stardew Valley)

From [Zelda Dungeon's Mabe Village page](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Mabe_Village) (a fan wiki):

- The village has "a central weathercock".
- Each building sits in its own quarter with its own use: the telephone booth and Ulrira's house to the south, the library at the south-western edge, the Trendy Game to the south-east, and the Dream Shrine and the Fishing Pond to the north.
- Every house belongs to a named household: Marin and Tarin's, Madam MeowMeow's, Ulrira's, the Quadruplets'.

From the official [Stardew Valley wiki's Pelican Town page](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Pelican_Town):

- Houses have addresses on named lanes: 1 Willow Lane, 2 Willow Lane, 1 River Road.
- "The river flowing through Pelican Town" is part of the town.
- The town opens on every side to a named place: the Bus Stop and the Farm, Cindersap Forest, the Beach, the Mountain.

What reads better there: no two buildings in the town are the same, and each one's place is explained by who lives there or what it is for. The layout is a set of quarters round one landmark, not rows along an axis. Crossroads Town already has the landmark (the well and the square) and the river. What it lacks is the variety.

What NapAttack should change (Claude E, decorative only):

1. Break Driftwood's 2 x 2 cottage block (finding 5). Use at least two cottage forms. Set each one by its household's trade:
   - the fisher's cottage nearest the quay, with nets and creels;
   - one on the river lane with a footbridge to its door;
   - one set back behind a garden wall with a vegetable bed.
2. Give the road one gentle bend at the square and one at the drop to the quay. Let the lanes to the cottages leave it at Y junctions and follow the river and the coast. This would give two lanes a player could name, as Pelican Town's are.
3. Give each household one object that says who lives there: the washing line already does this for one cottage.

### Hyrule Castle (A Link to the Past)

From [Zelda Wiki's Hyrule Castle page](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Hyrule_Castle) (a community wiki):

- The castle sits in the centre of the overworld, ringed by a moat that is "impassable".
- It is "inaccessible from a direct approach".
- Its courtyard is "filled with grass, plus Rosehedge and Orangecap bushes". The way in that the game hands the player is a hole under a bush in that courtyard, leading to a secret passage.

What reads better there: there is one clear front, the moat never breaks, and every other way in is a deliberate, drawn feature. The courtyard is also a soft place, inside hard walls.

Hearthold already has the unbroken moat and a strong south front with its bridge and banners. Its weak points are these:

- The north way in is an ambiguous second front (finding 4).
- The courtyard is paving from wall to wall, apart from the well, the training dummy and the barrels.

What NapAttack should change (Claude D, decorative only):

1. Make the north way a postern that reads as a secret or service gate: narrow, plain, with a dark passage through the parapet where the bridge lands, and steps down into the courtyard. Or remove it (finding 4).
2. Give the courtyard one soft corner suited to a stone castle in fire country: a stable yard with straw and a water trough by the west towers, or a raised herb bed against the keep's sunny wall. Leave the centre open as the node.
