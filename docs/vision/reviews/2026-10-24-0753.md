## Comparison: Gullwick against Stardew Valley's Beach and Pokémon's Dewford Town

Earlier runs compared:

- the coast, the isles, Neon and Cogsworth;
- the Frostline, the town, Driftwood, Hearthold and the Woodland;
- the home garden, the Brackens, Sunstone's rail lines and Cinder Springs.

This run takes Gullwick. It has a brief and no plan yet, so this is the cheapest time to change it.

I have not played these games for this note, and the claims stay at what the sources say.

### Sources

- **Stardew Valley,** [The Beach on the official wiki](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/The_Beach):
  - Willy's Fish Shop "is located on the main docks";
  - Elliott's cabin is "on the main beach, just east of the entrance";
  - the tide pools "are found at the east end of the beach", across a footbridge over the river.
- **Pokémon Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald,** [Dewford Town on Bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Dewford_Town):
  - "A tiny island in the blue sea";
  - "sandy shores and warm waters make it an ideal surf and fishing spot";
  - "Dewford houses a dock", and the houses and the Pokémon Center stand round the pier.

### What reads better there

- **The one structure on the water says what the place is for.** Stardew's fish shop stands on the docks, and Dewford's dock is the reason to land there. In Gullwick's brief, nothing reaches into the water: a cove with no jetty, slip or boat on the sand reads as an empty beach with houses.
- **The beach has parts with different uses.** Stardew's sand runs from the entrance to the cabin, then the dock, then the tide pools across a footbridge at the far end. Each part has its own use, so the place reads at a glance. Gullwick's markers are spread evenly, and they are the same generic set as every route (boots, marker stones, tracks).
- **The place is small and complete.** Dewford is "tiny", but it has a dock, houses and its one landmark. Gullwick at 420 x 220 is a similar size. That is enough for a slip, three or four cottages and a single landmark, if each slot is used for the cove's trade.

### What Gullwick should do (Claude I; decorative only, no new rules or rewards)

1. **Make it a beach landing, not a harbour, so that it differs from Driftwood.** Boats are hauled up on the shingle, as the brief's "a beach to haul boats" says. Put a capstan or winch at the top of the beach and two or three boats drawn up on rollers, with no jetty or quay. Driftwood keeps the jetties and the market.
2. **Give the cove one landmark on the shore:** a net loft or a fish-smoking hut with a tarred roof, at the head of the slip. That is Gullwick's "fish shop on the docks".
3. **Swap the generic story props for the cove's own:** a net hung to dry, lobster pots, an upturned hull used as a shed and a fish rack. Use the slots of the two old boots, one marker stone and one set of tracks. Keep one old boot, which suits a beach.
4. **Zone the cove from end to end:** the Coast Path comes in from the west at (7648, 5032) past the cottages, then the slip and the loft, then rocks at the east end. Put the heron pools (8129, 4982) there as tide pools, as Stardew does.
5. **Move the "fishing stage" from (8477, 4607) into the cove,** or rename it as something the Coast Path needs at that spot (lane G).
