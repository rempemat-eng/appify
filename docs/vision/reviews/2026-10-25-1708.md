## Findings, most severe first

### 1. After any zone change during play, the camera stops following the player, and the game cannot be played on

- **Where:** `game/game.gd` (the Phantom Camera binding) with `core/zones.gd` `go_to`. I hit it two ways:
  - **Through the door.** `--fresh --spawn=crossroads`, then walk east into `ToCrossroads` at (951, 1356) [(317, 452)]. Crossroads loads, but the screen shows another part of the blockout (the diagonal slope and the grey boxes), not the garden walk where the player should arrive (the `woodland` spawn, (90, 210) texels; compare `--zone=crossroads --spawn=woodland`, which shows him there). Bramble is nowhere on screen. Holding a direction for 3 s moves the view by about 40 screen px at most.
  - **By defeat.** Walk into the meadow camp east of the sign (mosslings at (570, 1095), (612, 1150) and (560, 1185)), and let it bring HP to 0. The defeat clip plays and `_on_downed` heals and sends the player to the woodland's `start`. The camera stays where he fell, with the mosslings back at home and HP shown full. The player is gone from view for good: 4 s of waiting and 4.5 s of walking changed nothing.
- **The likely cause, unverified:** `go_to` removes the player from the old zone's `Actors` and adds it to the new one. The startup `go_to` has the same reparent, and it works, so the difference is that a camera is already following. The likely culprit is the PhantomCamera2D losing or keeping a stale `follow_target` across `remove_child` and `add_child`. `docs/godot.md` lists this family of trap under "Node paths into an instanced scene".
- **Why it matters:** this breaks the slice's loop of woodland, Crossroads, the woodland, the Undercroft and the lantern. Every test passes because none renders a zone change with the camera live.
- **Owner:** Claude A (`game.gd`, `core/zones.gd`, the camera).
- **Action:**
  - Re-bind the camera's follow target (and `teleport_position()` to the player) after every `zone_changed`.
  - Add a GdUnit test: change zone twice, then wait some physics frames, and check that the camera's position is within a few texels of the player's (with the zone's limits allowed for).

### 3. At density 3, every tree and bush crown is a lace of fragments, and the ground shows through it

- **Where:** `game/zones/woodland/art`. In the game, the canopies read as a camouflage pattern over the grass. A bush reads as three brown stakes, for example at screen (1775 to 1840, 400) at the `stones` spawn.
- **How much is opaque** (the share of each picture's box that is drawn):

  | Picture | At density 3 (in the game) | At density 2 (`assets/zones/woodland/2x` on `claude-a/woodland-2x`) |
  | ------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | oak     | 0.31                       | 0.48                                                                |
  | beech   | 0.29                       | 0.43                                                                |
  | birch   | 0.20                       | 0.32                                                                |

  The other crowns at density 3: pine 0.25, willow 0.37, holly 0.25, bush 0.29 and bramble 0.24. At density 2 the crowns are solid masses of clumps. At density 3 only the clumps' rims and a few fills are left.

- **Where it comes from:** the kit's density 3 path. `python3 -m scripts.art.native.zone --zone woodland --density 3` on `claude-a/woodland-2x` writes pictures byte-identical to the game's, so the importer is not the cause. The likely place is the canopy painter's clump and noise thresholds, which are not scaled for 3.
- **Why it matters:** Slynyrd's tree method (below) builds a crown as layered, lit bundles that make "a convincing 3D mass". A see-through crown has no mass, no lit side and no shaded side, and the ground's grass tufts show through it. This is the "noisy" look that `eastward-edges.md` retires.
- **Owners:** Claude A (the kit's density 3 path and the woodland import), with Claude B for the prop painters.
- **Action:**
  - Fix the crown at density 3, so that it is as opaque as at density 2, within 0.05.
  - Add an opacity floor per kind to the kit's checks, so that this cannot come back silently.
  - Then re-import the woodland.

## Comparison: the valley woodland against Stardew's Cindersap Forest, ALTTP and Slynyrd's trees

The district this run is the woodland, because it is the first zone in finished art in the engine that will ship. I have not played these games for this note, and the claims stay at what the sources say.

### Sources

- **Stardew Valley Wiki, [Cindersap Forest](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Forest):**
  - it has four exits: the Farm to the north, Pelican Town to the east, the Sewers to the south, and the Secret Woods to the northwest, behind "a Large Log";
  - it "is bordered to the south by the ocean", with cliffs.
- **The repository's benchmark** (`research/world-benchmark.md`, 2.2):
  - Stardew: "Each map ends in a canopy of trees or cliffs; exits are few".
  - ALTTP: the Lost Woods have a "solid canopy", and the cliff rims are "stacked two or three deep".
- **Raymond Schlitter (Slynyrd), [Pixelblog 44: Top Down Trees](https://slynyrd.com/blog/2023/5/22/pixelblog-44-top-down-trees):**
  - a crown is built from repeated bundles of leaves, shaded like a sphere, using four or five colours per bundle from one ramp;
  - the bundles are layered by the light to make "a convincing 3D mass";
  - "The repetition of similar forms is key to maximize the clarity of the texture";
  - a trunk is a cylinder with bark stripes, widest at the centre.

### What reads better there

- **The edge is a thing, not the end of the picture.** Cindersap's edges are the ocean, cliffs and forest canopy, and each exit is a gap in them, sometimes with a physical gate (the Large Log). Our woodland's edges are grass that runs out of frame, and the player can walk through them (findings 2 and 7).
- **The crowns are solid.** In Slynyrd's method, a crown is a lit mass of bundles with a dark crescent at the bottom left. In ALTTP, the Lost Woods' canopy is solid enough to be a wall. Our crowns at density 3 are open lace, and the bushes read as stakes (finding 3). Once the crowns are solid, they are what makes a tree-wall border possible.
- **The trunks carry the tree.** Slynyrd's trunk is a lit cylinder joined to its crown. Our birch has a broken stripe for a trunk, and the beech has a gap (finding 4).
- **Few exits, each one obvious.** Cindersap has four exits, all on paths. The woodland has two doors (east and the hatch). Neither is marked in the art, and the hatch is a flat dark square (finding 6).

### What NapAttack should do (small actions; no new rules, rewards or mechanics)

1. **Make the woodland's frame:**
   - a tree wall of 3 or more species, 2 to 6 deep, on the west, south and north, using the fixed crowns;
   - the river leaving through a drawn outlet;
   - one opening east where the path narrows to the garden walk's 24 px (Claude E, then Claude A imports it).
2. **Gate the hatch physically:** draw a barred grate that the forge's restore unbars, in the way Stardew's log blocks the Secret Woods (Claude A, with the art from Claude B).
3. **Copy Slynyrd's crown check into the kit:** an opacity floor for each tree, and a lit-side and shade-side value step test for each crown, run at the density the game uses (Claude A and Claude B).
