## Comparison: Driftwood Harbour and the Tidebloom Isles

This run compares the valley coast and the isles (the previous run covered Sunstone). I have not played these games for this note. Claims stay at what the sources say.

### Stardew Valley's beach beside Driftwood Harbour

The official wiki ([Stardew Valley Wiki, "The Beach"](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/The_Beach)) records:

- The beach lies "to the south of Pelican Town", with Elliott's cabin east of the entrance.
- The main docks run out to the south, and Willy's Fish Shop stands "out on the docks".
- At "the easternmost edge of the main beach" a broken bridge leads to the tidal pools, which have a small dock of their own.

What reads better there:

- **Each structure is where its use is.** The fish shop stands on the dock it serves. Each dock is a destination, not a strip that stops in the water.
- **The beach is the transition.** Town becomes sand, sand becomes sea, along the whole south edge. Water and sand divide the parts of the beach; walls do not.
- **One small piece of the coast is held back behind a crossing.** It gives the place a second pocket.

What NapAttack should change at Driftwood (Claude E, with Claude B for sprites):

1. Put the fish market on the quay's root, facing the jetties, and end each jetty at a moored boat with a ladder. V3's ferry pier then becomes the one long pier with the ferry at its head.
2. Run sand round the harbour's whole inner shore, not only the west strip, so the sea always meets a beach or a quay, never a straight cut.
3. Make the breakwater a curved rubble mole from the Headland's cliff foot, with the lighthouse at its head on its own rock. This also answers fault 10.
4. Give the Headland's cliff path a small second pocket: a cove below the lookout reached by a short stair, with a rock pool. It is held back the way the tidal pools are, but stays decorative (AGENTS.md: no new rules).

### Link's Awakening's Koholint, and Sea of Stars, beside the Tidebloom Isles

[Zelda Wiki, "Tal Tal Mountain Range"](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Tal_Tal_Mountain_Range):

- The range lies at Koholint's "northernmost edge".
- It is "the main water source for the island", with several waterfalls falling from its eastern side.
- Tal Tal Heights lies directly south as "its main entrance".

[Zelda Wiki, "Toronbo Shores"](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Toronbo_Shores): where you arrive is a beach in the island's south-west, with "tall palm trees for shade in the sun", bordering the village region to the north.

Thierry Boulanger of Sabotage, on Sea of Stars' level design ([MobileSyrup, 11 Sep 2023](https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/09/11/sea-of-stars-level-design-sabotage-studio-thierry-boulanger-interview/)):

- The game moves "seamlessly in and out of water".
- Its maps are "all these small pockets".

What reads better there:

- **The island is organised by landforms, not by screens.** Highlands give water and sit at one edge; a named entry area leads up into them; the arrival beach is a wide open shore at the opposite corner. Tidebloom already has the same parts:
  - the Headwaters' falls;
  - the switchback;
  - Lagoon Landing in the south-west, which matches Toronbo's position.

  The difference is that Tidebloom's parts are bounded by the grid lines (finding 3).
- **Small pockets.** Each pocket is a self-contained moment: a pool under a fall, a cove, a ledge with a view. Tidebloom's Rainforest and Cove are large, evenly filled fields of trees and palms (rule 12).

What NapAttack should change in the isles (Claude C):

1. Name and shape the Headwaters as the island's highland at the north-east edge. Its rim follows a contour and sends two falls down its side: the existing one, plus a smaller seasonal one into the Rainforest. Each fall gets a plunge pool and spray.
2. Turn the switchback's recess into the "main entrance" of the highland: a framed notch with a viewpoint bench at the top that looks back down over the island.
3. Thin the Rainforest's rows of big trees along the west coast (several stacked in one column, rule 8). Group the canopy trees into two or three groves, with a clearing pocket between them on the river: a fallen log over the stream and a pool to look into.
4. Make the black-sand Cove a pocket rather than a strip: a basalt arm round it, the canoe drawn up, and one sea stack offshore with gulls. This replaces the hole-like islets (finding 8).
