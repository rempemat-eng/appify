## Findings, most severe first

### 1. The preview shows two pixel sizes on one screen. Its notes say the opposite

- **Where:** #242, `prototypes/mygame-world-preview/`. You can see it on every screen of the preview. The clearest view is at 1920 x 1080 (`?fhd=1`) with any character beside a pine.
- **What I measured:**

  | Picture                                                      | What its texels look like                                 |
  | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
  | `world/ground.png` (960 x 540)                               | 100 % of its 2 x 2 blocks are one flat colour             |
  | `world/pine.png`, `oak.png`, `boulder.png`                   | 100 % of their 4 x 4 blocks are one flat colour           |
  | The cast's frames (`bramble/E/walk.png`, `cairn/E/idle.png`) | only 32 to 41 % of their 2 x 2 blocks are one flat colour |

  So the world is drawn at one texel per world px. On screen at 1920 x 1080, each of its texels is 4 x 4 screen px. The cast is drawn at two texels per world px, and each of its texels is 2 x 2 screen px.

- **What the player sees:** Bramble's face, belt and bow are drawn at half the pixel size of the pine beside him. The pine's stepped edges are twice as coarse as his outline.
- **Why it matters:**
  - `art-style.md` "Density 2" asks for one density for all new art.
  - Lane R's brief lists "two pixel sizes" as an art fault to report.
  - #242's handoff (`docs/codex/mygame-character-integration.md`) says "The existing terrain and new character source texels share that scale". They share the canvas, but not the texel.
  - Matt is about to judge the "pixel grid or HD" options partly on how the cast looks in the world. This preview would show him a mix that neither option intends.
- **Owners:** Codex (#242) for the preview and its note. Claude A for the options going to Matt.
- **Action:**
  - **Codex:** correct the sentence in the handoff.
  - **Codex:** add a line to the preview's status bar that measures and shows the texel size of the ground, the props and the character, using the same block test as `npm run check:art-density`.
  - **Claude A:** in the options for Matt, show each one with the world and the cast on **one** texel grid. Either the cast at the world's density, or a world scene at the cast's. Don't use this mixed frame.

## Comparison: the cast in the world, against Eastward, Slynyrd's sprite guide and Sea of Stars

Earlier runs compared districts. This run compares how a hero meets the world, because that is the decision on the table. I have not played these games for this note, and the claims stay at what the sources say.

### Sources

- **Eastward:** the repository's own measurements (`eastward-edges.md`, from the official references). John is 54 native px, shown as 162 screen px in a 1080 frame. One tile is 17 native px, laid in pairs of 34.
- **Raymond Schlitter, [Pixelblog 22: Top Down Character Sprites](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2019/10/21/pixelblog-22-top-down-character-sprites)** (`art-style.md` already cites his Pixelblogs 20 and 21):
  - "tile size is the best gauge for determining sprite size";
  - "1 tile wide by 2 tiles tall" is a common choice;
  - native resolutions of 320 x 180, 480 x 270 and 640 x 360 scale to 1920 x 1080;
  - buildings should "have a believable scale in relation to the character sprites".
- **Sea of Stars,** [MegaVisions, "The Art of Sea of Stars"](https://www.megavisions.net/the-art-of-sea-of-stars-a-sea-of-pixels/): it "looks like a 90s RPG rendered in HD", with "a larger color palette", yet you "can count every single pixel".

### What reads better there

- **One grid for everyone.** In each of these, the hero and the ground are drawn on the same pixel. Sea of Stars is the case nearest to an "HD" choice, and it still keeps a countable pixel across characters and scenery. It widens the palette, not the pixel. Our preview mixes 2 x 2 and 4 x 4 screen texels (finding 1).
- **The hero sets the tile, not the other way round.** Slynyrd sizes the sprite from the tile, about 2 tiles tall. Eastward's John is about 3 tiles of 17 tall. Our cast at 48 world px is 6 of our 8 px cells tall. The world's doors, storeys and soft grid were sized for 3 cells (finding 2).
- **Buildings at a believable scale to the hero.** Slynyrd prefers that to the shrunken-building convention. A 32 px door against a 48 px hero is a shrunken building, which is the opposite of what the size guide set out to do.
- **Fewer colours carry more.** The world's props already work this way, with 14 to 17 colours, while the cast's frames have thousands (finding 3).

### What NapAttack should do (decisions for Matt; no new rules, rewards or mechanics)

1. **Show Matt each option on one grid** (Claude A and Codex). Either Bramble at 50 art px on a density 2 kit scene, or a world crop redrawn to the cast's resolution. Use the same framing for both: 480 x 270 world px at 1920 x 1080, so each texel is a whole 4 x 4 or 2 x 2 block of screen pixels.
2. **Fix the hero's height in cells first** (Claude A). Write the answer into `size-guide.md` as "an adult is N cells". Then let doors (about 1.3 adults), storeys and the soft grid follow from it, as the guide already derives them from 25.
3. **Give the cast the world's ramps if the pixel grid wins** (Codex). Add a per-material tone count to the studio's review tool, using the same test as `art-style.md`'s checklist.
