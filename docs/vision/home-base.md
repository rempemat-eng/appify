# The home base: a home that grows while you rest

Matt, 20 Oct 21:38: "i want you to be able to upgrade your home base. not just customize your house from the inside but your whole base (house from the outside, e.g. build it into a bigger house of a different type perhaps, your garden, the pond, change what elements are part of your base, in a vague sense like clash of clans)".

**Status: a proposal. Nothing here is built, and nothing changes a rule until Matt says yes.** Every new mechanic or rule change is marked **PROPOSAL** with an id (HB1 to HB14) so the decisions in section 18 can point at it. The ten decisions continue the numbering of `gameplay-regions.md` (PR #143, decisions 1 to 12) as 13 to 22, and are also in `decisions.json` for the answer page (Claude A's scratchpad). The currency is **Dream Dust** (renamed from GRASS by Matt, 20 Oct 21:29; the code still says `grass`). Numbers are **real minutes**: the game still runs at demo speed, one second for a minute (`lib/offline-rules.ts`). AGENTS.md still holds: content and mechanics come after the art, motion and HUD pass, so section 17 orders this work behind it.

It builds on, and does not repeat: `garden-growth.md`, `home-hub.md`, `living-world-plan.md` (L2 currency and care, L3 decay, L7 decorating), the decorating model (C12, `lib/decor.ts`), `gameplay-regions.md` (#143: rest spots G1, finds G5, comforts G9, how rest pays G10, decay at home only G16, the balance sketch), the world plan v5.3 and the home valley brief (#96), the house style and its kits (#121), the soft grid (`verticality.md`, the terrace toolkit #90) and the size guide (#124).

## 0. In short

1. **The base is a plot on the soft grid that grows.** Today's garden (320 x 576) is its core and never shrinks. Beside it on the terrace, the copse (west, 64 px) and the brook meadow (east, 96 px) start overgrown and open in stages; clearing them is a builder's job, like Clash of Clans' obstacles or Stardew's debris, and gives back what the overgrowth is made of. In the end the plot is 480 x 576, 60 x 72 cells (HB1).
2. **The hearth is the town hall.** Its level, one to six, opens the next parcel, the next house tier, new pieces and higher levels. Nothing else has to be maxed first (HB2).
3. **The house grows in tiers:** the nap hut, then a cottage, then a home of its own kind (a farmhouse, a mill house on its leat, a treehouse, a tower cottage), then extensions. Each tier adds rooms inside. Every part is a valley kit piece in a colourway; repainting and swapping unlocked parts is free (HB3).
4. **Twelve kinds of pieces** to place, move and upgrade: beds, the pond in four tiers, the orchard, the workshop, the greenhouse, beehives, the hammock grove, the bathhouse, the bird hide, the dock, the observatory and the reading nook, plus paths, fences, hedges, lamps and benches. Each does something: a place to rest, a lean on which visitors come, a find it shelters (HB4, HB8, HB9).
5. **Builders work while you rest.** Every minute of a nap or outing, day or night, is a builder's minute; nothing moves while you are on the phone, and nothing can be bought to finish early. A queue means a long sleep can finish several jobs and no one has to come back to restart idle builders (HB5).
6. **Costs are Dream Dust plus materials** found in the regions: ten materials, one or two per region, brought back by rests at that region's found spots. Moss sells any material of a region you have found at a premium, so travelling is never required (HB6).
7. **A touch-first layout editor:** the 8 px grid, footprints from the size guide, 44 px handles, free and instant moves, storage that keeps levels, undo (HB7).
8. **Safe by construction:** raids never touch the base (HB10); decay stays visual, capped and per home, and never undoes growth (HB11); nothing is daily, seasonal-only, sold for money or lost.
9. **Pacing:** the whole base costs about 7,600 Dream Dust and about 74 hours of rest. A regular player reaches the picture's second stage in about two weeks and its third in about two months, and finishes in about four and a half months; a committed player in about a third of the time, a light player in about 1.6 times as long (section 14).
10. **Ten decisions for Matt** (section 18).

**What makes base building satisfying,** across these: a visible next step; growth you can see from across the screen; a reveal after time away; your own layout; materials that come from the world around you; a person who builds it.

**What makes it predatory,** across these: time sold for money (gems, leaf tickets, diamonds); builders sold; no queue, so idle builders summon you; respawning chores; storage that fills and stops; loot and raids; shields and countdowns; limited offers.

## 3. Principles

1. **Rest is the engine.** Building moves only with rest, as the garden grows only with rest (`garden-growth.md`). Taps never build.
2. **Nothing is ever lost.** Cleared ground stays clear; levels never drop; storing a piece keeps its level; switching house type keeps everything; a cancelled job refunds in full.
3. **Nothing is sold for money, and time is never for sale.** No speed-ups, no builders for money, no premium currency (#95's no-paywall rule).
4. **Nothing to do every day.** The builders wait; a queue means no one has to come back to restart them; nothing expires.
5. **The base is never at risk.** Raids never touch it; decay is visual, capped and reversible (L3), and does not grow with the base.
6. **The world stays logical.** The house grows in place; water keeps its source and sink; paths lead somewhere; fences end on something (world-rules); every piece is a kit piece on the soft grid at the size guide's scale.
7. **Moving is decorating.** Moving, turning, storing, repainting and swapping unlocked parts are free and instant (AGENTS.md: decorative interactions add no rewards, costs or chores; B1's precedent).
8. **One currency.** Dream Dust stays the only money. Materials are not a currency: they cannot be earned by play, sold or traded, never expire and have no cap.
9. **Phones first.** Touch-first editing, 44 px targets, reduced motion, image memory within `check:images`, nothing DOM-based outside `.web` files.

- **Why there.** The brook already runs through all three parcels from the woodland's spring pool to the millpond, so the larger plot is one place, not three. The house, the gate, the bridge and the trail stay where they are, so the hub's doors, journeys and harness hold (world plan 8.2 keeps the garden's size and place because "the hub, the harness and every save point depend on it").
- **What it costs the valley (HB14).** The woodland gives up 64 px (416 to 352 wide) and the riverside 96 px (544 to 448); the world plan's v5.4 would reserve them and Claude E would draw the plot's edge into the woodland's fade and the riverside's hedge. The lookout step needs 160 x 96 of the Frostline's slope beside the trail; it is optional and last. If Matt prefers not to move the valley (decision 14), the plot stays 320 x 576, with overgrowth drawn into its own edges and room for about half the pieces.
- **Clearing** is a builder's job (5 to 15 Dream Dust, 5 to 15 rest minutes) chosen by tapping the obstacle. It never respawns (Clash of Clans' vegetation and Stardew's weeds do), never pays a random prize, and gives back what it is made of: about 30 timber and 30 stone in all, enough for the first two hearths. Healthy trees and bushes are not obstacles; they are kept or moved.
- **The boundary** follows the parcels as they open: the hedge, fence or drystone wall along the plot's west, south and east edges is a kit run that ends on something (a gate post, the brook's bank, a tree), and never crosses water.

## 8. Building while you rest (HB5, HB13)

**HB5. Builders work in rest minutes. PROPOSAL (new mechanic).**

- **Who.** The builders are a valley resident's crew: the forester from the woodland's yard (world plan 8.2) and an apprentice, with their sawhorse and ladder. They are people with lines and routines (C11), not a resource.
- **What counts.** Every minute of a nap or an outing, day or night, between START and WAKE UP is a builder's minute. Awake minutes are not. Opening the app mid-rest changes nothing (#143, 6.2). Nights count in full, so a night's sleep matters to the base even though G10 pays it a flat gift instead of per minute.
- **The queue.** One crew works one job at a time, in the order the player set; the queue holds three jobs, five with the workshop at level 2, and a second crew works alongside at level 3. A long sleep can finish several queued jobs. Clash of Clans' builders cannot take a queued job at all; here no one has to come back to restart them.
- **Paying.** A job is paid when it joins the queue (spending, so inside the 3-minute play budget like hats and levels). Cancelling refunds everything. Reordering is free.
- **No speed-ups.** Nothing finishes a job early: no Dream Dust, no money, no ads, no watching.
- **Progress** is shown as rest still to come ("about two more naps", or "about 40 minutes of rest"), on the builders' board by the gate and on the plan, never as a clock that ticks while the player is on the phone.
- **Under construction**, a piece stands marked out with stakes and string, then scaffolded; the companion and paths walk round it; it can still be moved.
- **Motion.** The crew works in short, finite loops while the screen is active (hammer, carry, look), still with reduced motion, stopped when the screen is inactive (AGENTS.md).

**HB13. The welcome back shows what was built. PROPOSAL (presentation).** When a job has finished during a rest, the welcome back gives it one line ("the greenhouse is finished.") and, on closing, the hub's camera eases to it while the scaffold comes down and the companion goes to look (anticipate, act, settle; a still with reduced motion). Several finished jobs show as one short list, never a backlog to click through. No push notification is sent for a finished job.

## 10. The layout editor (HB7)

**HB7. The layout editor. PROPOSAL (extends C12's decorating).**

- **Opening it.** Tap the builders' board by the gate, or BUILD in the decorate panel (a new tab beside the keepsake chest, Claude B's `components/decorate-panel.tsx`). Only when the companion is awake and not on a mission, nap or battle.
- **The view.** The hub's camera eases out to frame the plot's part around the companion (no zoom change: `ART_ACROSS` stays 300); the soft grid shows as faint dots at cell corners; open parcels are outlined, closed ones dimmed with the hearth they need; the HUD and hotbar stay put.
- **Select.** A tap on a piece lifts it (a 2 px shadow) and shows its handles: MOVE, TURN (where it reads), STORE and UPGRADE (its card: next level, price, rest minutes, what it does). Each handle is at least 34 x 34 art px (44 screen px), placed outside the piece on the side away from the HUD and the screen's edge.
- **Move.** Drag the piece or its MOVE handle; it rides 24 px above the finger so the finger never hides it, snaps to 8 px cells, and its footprint lights green, or amber with one reason word (wall, path, water, door, too close, cut off). A haptic tick on each snap (`expo-haptics`). Let go where it fits and it settles; where it does not, it slides back to the last cell that fitted (instant with reduced motion).
- **Runs.** Paths, fences and hedges are drawn as runs: drag from cell to cell and the kit derives corners, posts, gate posts and edges (Townscaper's "large shapes"). Paths are orthogonal runs of 16 or 24 px with rounded corners (soft grid).
- **Undo and redo** for the session's changes; DONE saves, CANCEL restores the layout as it was.
- **Storage.** STORE puts a piece in the shed with its level; placing it again is free and instant.
- **New pieces** come from the BUILD catalogue (only what the hearth allows); place it where it fits and it is marked out, and its job joins the queue (HB5).
- **What does not move:** the house (it grows in place), the water (the brook, the pond's basin, the leat), the bridge, the gate (on the square's axis) and the trail, and Moss's stall (not the player's). In phase 3 (section 17) the service pieces with hotspots (mailbox, signpost, bowl, lantern, nook, watering can) become movable once hotspots are read from the layout.
- **The checks,** extending `checkDecor` from 4 px walk cells to 8 px pieces: the footprint lies on one tier and not on a face, stair, landing or lip; not on water (the dock only on a water edge); door aprons (4 x 2 cells) clear; trees one cell from a path; every keep spot reachable from the gate (the door, the bed, the trail, the bridge, every hotspot and rest spot); a quiet clearing of at least 6 x 6 cells kept near where the companion wakes (AGENTS.md, size guide); hit boxes at least 34 x 34 art px and apart at standing distance; fences and hedges end on something and never cross water; the house's parts pass `logic()`.

## 11. How it fits the rest

- **Decay (HB11. PROPOSAL, scope of the approved L3 and #143's G16).** Decay lives only at home (G16), so the base is where it shows: weeds on paths and in beds, leaves on the pond, moss on a roof, a fallen fence rail, a lamp out, drawn as layers on the pieces. It stays visual, capped and reversible (L3). It never lowers a level, never undoes a clearing (overgrowth never returns), never pauses the builders and never closes a rest spot. Health is measured once for the home, not per piece, so the repair (a tidy-up job the player chooses, with L3's visible Dream Dust cost) costs the same for a hut as for a manor: a larger base is never more work.
- **Raids (HB10. PROPOSAL, new rule).** Raids never touch the base: no damage, no theft, no defences, no shields, no revenge. A raid stays a card battle about the companion (and, with L2, leaves it worn out). The base is never shown to a raider. Friend visits (L9, multiplayer, last) could later show a base read-only, with Matt's go-ahead.
- **The companion.** It walks the base, uses pieces in its idle moments (naps in the hammock, dips its toes off the dock, watches the builders), and says one line when a job finishes. It never mentions idle builders, prices or what is missing. In the editor it steps aside to the quiet clearing.
- **Visitors.** HB9's leans and garden visitors; a befriended visitor can stay in the guest room (presentation).
- **Garden growth.** Each bed and tree keeps the lifetime rest minute it was planted (`since`) and grows on the existing schedule from then (a stage every 15 minutes for beds; +30, +120, +300, +600 for trees). Today's six beds and three pits keep their sowing minutes, so nothing changes for them. Upgrading never resets growth. Growth stays presentation only and never wilts.
- **The hub.** The base is the garden tile of the one world (`home-hub.md`). The wider plot moves `gardenOrigin` 64 px west in `lib/home-hub.ts`; after C29 (saves on the world canvas) the plot's origin is a world point and pieces are saved relative to it, so the canvas move and the base do not collide.
- **Rests and the economy.** Building spends Dream Dust inside the play budget like any purchase; moving is free and outside it. Nothing on the base earns Dream Dust (decision 19). G10's daily ceiling and well-rested gift are untouched.
- **Ways and regions.** A region's material arrives with its way (one a day at most). The base never gates a way, and no way gates the base beyond its material.

## 14. Pacing and numbers

**How it fits #143's balance sketch.** #143 found that GRASS (Dream Dust) runs out of uses once hats are bought, and proposed comforts (about 4,000 to 5,000) and outfits (about 1,200) as the long tail. The base adds about 7,600, the largest single sink, and the only one that changes the home. With both, a committed player has about two months of things to fund and a light player about ten months; that is acceptable only because nothing is lost by going slower and every stage is a finished, pretty home. If Matt wants it shorter, halving every price brings the light player's base to under four months. **C33** (the economy simulation, on the Codex queue) should add the base as a sink so these numbers are checked by simulation, not by hand.

**Materials pace.** The whole base needs about 440 materials, about 60 of them from clearing. At 25 a week the regular player has the rest in about 15 weeks, before the Dream Dust; the committed player (about 50 a week) in about 8 weeks and the light player (about 10) in about 38, a little after the Dream Dust, which is where Moss's premium helps. So the last row can take a few weeks longer for committed and light players who never buy from Moss.

## 15. Anti-patterns to avoid

| Anti-pattern                | Where it could creep in                                         | Guard                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pay to skip                 | a "finish now" button, ads to speed up                          | none exists; time is only rest (HB5)                                  |
| Playing by appointment      | idle builders, crops that spoil, collectors that fill           | a queue; nothing produces; nothing spoils                             |
| Chores                      | weeds that come back, upkeep that grows with the base           | clearing is once; decay per home, capped (HB11)                       |
| Loss                        | raids, decay that lowers levels, demolition                     | raids never touch the base; levels never drop; store, never demolish  |
| Fear of missing out         | limited pieces, seasonal sets, events                           | every piece every season (G15); no offers                             |
| Debt                        | loans for the house                                             | pay as you go                                                         |
| Grind                       | farming materials with short naps                               | materials scale with minutes, not rests (HB6)                         |
| Pressure from the companion | "the builders are waiting", "your neighbours have a greenhouse" | the companion never mentions prices, idle builders or what is missing |
| Comparison                  | leaderboards of bases, ratings                                  | none (no new social feature without Matt)                             |
| Paywall                     | materials or builders for money                                 | none, unless Matt decides otherwise                                   |
