# World atlas: the places beyond the valley

Matt, 17 Oct (20:22 and 20:23): more places ("cyber punk or steam punk, a desert/western town, lava themed, maybe out of space … a space station or another planet", "a castle or harbour like in the picture"), seamless transitions between places, and a world that feels alive. This file is the direction; the order of work is in `living-world-plan.md`. Everything here follows the world logic rules there and the art rules in `coherence-plan.md`.

## The idea that ties it together

NapAttack is about resting instead of scrolling, so the world is laid out as a journey from rest to screens and back to sleep. Near home everything is quiet, green and slow. The farther you travel, the more the places run on machines, glare and noise, until the neon city that never sleeps, home of Doomscroll Dave. Beyond it, the deepest rest: a quiet moon above the clouds. Every region has a resident who has lost their balance in that region's way, and a sparring ring where you meet them.

## The connected world (Matt, 18 Oct 19:07)

Matt: "i really want this world to be immersive. you shouldnt sacrifice immersiveness to save compute. so i would like you to roughly follow what codex drew up." The reference is Codex's concept map and logical map in draft PR #23 (`docs/codex/ideas/connected-world/`): one continent, with places joined on foot wherever the land joins them, and journeys only where the land does not (rail, maglev, lift, shuttle, and a ferry to the islands).

- **Sunstone Reach (done, 18 Oct):** the desert and the fire country are one walk. Palm Oasis, Dust Gulch, then the Scorched Pass east of the gulch where the sand gives way to ash, Hearthold north of it over its long bridge, and the caldera and Cinder Springs beyond. The mine cart still runs from the gulch to the caldera's cart bay as a quick way there. Region id `west`; the stops are `west` (the desert line) and `lava` (the cart bay).
- **Frostline Highlands (done, 18 Oct):** above the Ridge cabin, part of the valley's own walk. The ridge's terrace and face run on west into **Fir Hollow** (old firs under the face, no snow down there; its foot closes over the Woodland, no way down); a gap in the peaks leads north to **Snowmelt Lake** in its cirque (a frozen fall on the wall, ice at the rim); the south shore path goes east to **the Snow Saddle** (a stone bothy, a windsock, the fork), which drops through the ridge's own gap back to the cabin; north of the saddle a spur climbs to **the High Lookout** (a viewing scope, a windbreak, a bench). Zone ids `hollow`, `snowmelt`, `saddle`, `summit`; the ways through are fixed spans in `zones.py` (`RIDGE_COL`, `HOLLOW_COL`, `LAKE_PORT`, `SUMMIT_COL`), the same on both sides. Past the edges: the high peaks (`beyond_peaks()`). The ridge's mission is unchanged.
- **Tidebloom Isles (done, 18 Oct):** a dormant volcanic island off Driftwood Harbour, region `isles`, reached by **the Driftwood Ferry** from the harbour's jetty (stop `pier`) to the pier at **Lagoon Landing** (stop `isles`; a free, skippable journey like the train, over the sea with the island rising ahead). One loop on foot: the Landing (boathouse, pier, the lagoon's clear shallows), **the Rainforest** north of it (giants with buttress roots, tree ferns, a footbridge over the river), **the Headwaters** (a waterfall off the old volcano into a round pool; the river runs from it through the forest to the lagoon) and **Black-sand Cove** (surf on black volcanic sand, a turtle above the tide line), back west along the shore. The sea lies all round (`beyond_sea()`). Crossings are fixed in `zones.py` (`ISLE_TRAIL_*`, `ISLE_RIVER_*`); the coast and the south shore are single functions across zones (`isle_west_sea`, `isle_south_water`), so the water meets itself at every seam.
- **Saved places survive every change:** a saved spot records the layout it was saved in (`lib/world-layouts.ts`) and is carried to the same point of the same zone in the new world. The walked-zone list is kept as it is.
- **Journeys run between stops, not regions** (`StopId` in `lib/world-places.ts`), so a region can hold more than one.
- **Lava keeps its distance:** the walk grid leaves a cell beside lava at the sides, so the companion never looks as if it stands in it.

## Hearthold moves to the fire country (Matt, 18 Oct 16:33)

Matt: "the castle could be in the lava section. perhaps you go through the desert biome and at the end there is a stone bridge underneath which lava flows and around the castle is lava, perhaps with a dragon flying around." So:

- **The way there is walked, not ridden.** The desert and the fire country become one walkable region: Dust Gulch and the oasis, then a scorched stretch where the sand darkens to basalt and the first cracks glow, then a long stone bridge over a lava chasm (lava flowing under it, heat shimmer, no rail on the drop side), then Hearthold on a rock ringed by lava, reached only by that bridge. The caldera and the hot springs stay in the same region, past the castle. Real-world logic holds: lava runs downhill from the volcano round the castle's rock to the chasm, cools to crust at its edges, and nothing green grows near it.
- **The dragon is the castle's keeper, and it is scenery.** It circles the towers on a slow, finite loop and lands on the keep to rest; it grants nothing, costs nothing and never blocks a path (AGENTS.md). With reduced motion it sleeps curled on the keep.
- **The valley's corner becomes the Headland:** sea cliffs above the harbour's breakwater, a stone lookout tower with a timber top (the reference Matt sent), wildflowers, sheep and the cliff path's end. The harbour keeps its breakwater root and its sea.
- **Order:** the Headland replaces the castle zone in the valley; the castle is redrawn for its new ground; the desert and fire regions are joined (the mine cart line stays as a shortcut back); the world map shows the new layout.

## Every region is its own place (Matt, 17 Oct 20:52)

Matt: "make sure different worlds are as detailed as i instructed you to be and they are not just reskins of other worlds but are their own". So each region is designed from its own idea, not from a template:

- **Its own layout.** No shared plan of "buildings in a row, a square, a ring at the bottom". Crossroads Town is a square round a well; Cogsworth Junction is a railway platform above a cobbled square ruled by a clock tower, with an airship mast in the meadow; Dust Gulch is one street strung down from the railway's end, under a mesa, across a dry creek; Neon Harbour is a wet street between tower blocks and the quay.
- **Its own props.** A zone outside the valley draws only its own region's props (`REGION_PROPS` in `scripts/art/zones.py` fails the build otherwise); the only shared pieces are things found anywhere (steam, a drone, a fence post). A bench in Cogsworth is cast iron, in Neon Harbour it is a tram shelter, in Dust Gulch there is a hitching rail.
- **Its own ground.** Every region paints its own surfaces with the toolkit: cobbles and soot in Cogsworth, wind ripples, cracked hardpan and a mesa's strata in Dust Gulch, wet asphalt, puddles and neon reflections in Neon Harbour.
- **Its own story in the scene.** The details explain the place: the well in Dust Gulch is dry and the windpump still stands over it, the creek bed is cracked mud, the line stops at a buffer by the mesa; the train in Cogsworth is still steaming; the signs in Neon Harbour double in every puddle.
- **The valley's detail level.** Each prop gets the house's treatment: material ramps, light from the upper left, recesses, cast shadows, small story details. Concepts are reviewed side by side before any zone is wired.

## World logic for the atlas

- Walking zones join only where the ground could really meet: the valley and the harbour are one walkable region; the desert, the castle and the fire country another (18 Oct).
- Distant regions are joined by a vehicle that belongs to both ends: a railway from town, the airship from Cogsworth, a mine cart into the caldera, the maglev into the city, the space elevator out of it. The station or dock is drawn in both zones.
- Each region keeps its own rules: sand and cacti only in Dust Gulch and the oasis, lava only in the caldera (it flows downhill and cools to crust at its edges), rain and neon in Neon Harbour, snow on the ridge.
- Day and night are shared everywhere, except that Neon Harbour never gets fully dark or fully bright, and Lumen's sky is always starry.

## Seamless travel

- **Within a region, no loading and no cut.** Zones sit on one grid. When the companion walks toward an edge, the neighbouring zone is already mounted beside the current one, and the camera follows across the border in one continuous scroll. Only the current zone and its neighbours are kept in memory.
- **Borders are painted together.** The generators draw each shared border from the same seed, so the road leaving the garden's south gate is the very road entering Crossroads Town, and the grass, treeline and fences run straight across. A check compares the border strips of neighbouring zones (paths line up, terrain matches or blends).
- **Between regions, the journey is the transition.** A two to four second vehicle scene: the train pulls out, the landscape scrolls past in parallax layers as it changes from meadow to brass, the train pulls in. It can be skipped, plays once, and with reduced motion it is a still frame and a short cross-fade.
- **The view from the window belongs to where you are going (18 Oct).** Each journey's layers reuse the destination region's own props, hazed and shrunk with distance: snow-capped hills, hedgerows, a windmill and cottages, telegraph poles and a fence for the valley; chimneys, the clock tower and a canal with its towpath for Cogsworth; banded mesas, saguaros and a dry wash for the west; the volcano, basalt columns and a lava river for the fire country; the elevated line, holo boards and a wet street doubling the neon for the city. The band is 240 art pixels tall, so on a phone it fills most of the screen.
- **Trips follow the lines (18 Oct).** A station offers its own line's stops first; further places are listed with where you change (`travelRoute` in `lib/world-places.ts`: valley–Cogsworth, Cogsworth–Dust Gulch, Dust Gulch–caldera, Cogsworth–Neon Harbour, Neon Harbour–Halcyon, Halcyon–Lumen). A trip of several legs plays each leg a little quicker, labelled "TO COGSWORTH, THEN LUMEN", and one SKIP ends the whole trip. Each line has its own vehicle (`travelVehicle`): the steam train in the valley and out west, the maglev to and from the city, three coal tubs on the mine line, the elevator capsule to Halcyon and the shuttle to Lumen.
- **No holes in the map.** Where a region's grid has no zone (the valley's corners beside the ridge and west of the harbour), old forest closes the gap: a canopy of leaf masses in deep shade (`beyond()` in `scripts/art/zones.py`, `zoneBeyond`). The same land runs on past a region's edge when the screen sees past it (a one-row region on a tall phone): forest round the valley, the volcano's flank round the fire country (`beyond_ash()`), open dunes round Dust Gulch (`beyond_dunes()`). A region with no open country round it shows its own dark instead (night over Neon Harbour, space round Halcyon and Lumen, the room's dark at home; `BACKDROP` in `components/atlas-walk.tsx`), never the valley's green.
- **Interrupting is always safe.** Turning back mid-walk or leaving the app mid-journey never triggers a stale arrival, a nap or a navigation (AGENTS.md).

Every journey now leaves from a drawn stop: the valley train from Valley Halt, the Cogsworth train at the junction platform, the maglev between the Skyport and the atmospheric terminus on Cogsworth Canal, the mine cart in Dust Gulch, the elevator from the Skyport's anchor.

**Cogsworth Canal is the working side of the steam town, not the square again.** The junction is a cobbled square of workshops and a clock tower; the canal has water and industry: a towpath of setts, an iron bridge, a steam narrowboat by a jib crane, a lock with black-and-white balance beams, the foundry under its sawtooth roof with the pour glowing through its door, and the brass-and-glass terminus where the maglev from Neon arrives as an atmospheric railway (the two cities meeting in each other's style). At the bottom, allotments warmed by the foundry's waste steam, tended by a clockwork gardener. Its own props: narrowboat, canal crane, foundry, tube station, atmospheric car, automaton, cloches.

**Cinder Springs is where the fire country rests, not more lava field.** The caldera is crust, columns and the forge; the springs are a staircase of hot-spring terraces (milky turquoise pools held by scalloped cream travertine lips, orange mats round the hottest, each spilling into the next), a hot stream that hisses where it meets the end of the caldera's lava river, sulphur round the fumaroles, fireweed (the first plant back on burnt ground), and a lantern-lit path to a bathhouse of charred cedar. Its own props: bathhouse, fireweed, stone lanterns.

**Halcyon Station has rooms now.** Bulkheads with lit doorways divide the arrival hall (hydroponic troughs, hazard stripes at the elevator's airlock), the atrium (garden pods, the low-gravity ring), the sleep bay on soft carpet with lockers, the galley on warm deck boards (a round table, tea), the navigation consoles and the shuttle hatch, and the lounge under the viewports (benches, books, a telescope). The hull is closed: Lumen is its own region, reached by shuttle (the atlas plays the climb), never on foot through the wall.

**Lumen ends the journey in the Dream Grove.** Footprints wind down from the shuttle's scorched pad past boulders with hard airless shadows, rayed craters, a rille and a ring of crystals round the sparring crater, to a wide crater carpeted in glowing moss round the sleeping tree, with dream pools, glowing caps and a hammock slung between two crystal spires: the deepest rest. Its own props: moon tree, hammock, moon rocks, glow caps.

**The Skyport is where the city's noise stops.** Neon Harbour's wet street runs in and ends at a quiet drop-off loop round a tree; the dark tiles give way (an ordered dither over the first 56 pixels) to pale polished slabs. The maglev waits on a white guideway along the north; a concourse with departure pylons, ticket gates you walk through, benches and living trees leads to a pier and the anchor out in the harbour, where the elevator's ribbon climbs out of sight. Brighter and calmer than the street: the way up and out, in keeping with the journey from screens back to rest. Its own props: maglev, tether, ticket gates, departure pylons, pod trees, sky benches, luggage bots.

**The Desert Oasis is its own place, not the gulch again.** The gulch is a flat town on cracked hardpan; the oasis is a sea of sculpted dunes (lit windward faces with ripples, a sharp crest, a shaded slip face) round a hollow where a spring fills a clear pool, with stone steps, papyrus, a shaduf and palms. It explains the gulch: the gulch's dry creek was the pool's overflow until a dune crept over the outlet, and the drift still lies across the bed. The gulch's wagon track ends at a caravan camp (tent, resting camels, a fire with tea), the railway runs on west past a water crane piped from the spring, and an older town lies half-buried in the south-west (you walk through its arch). Its own props: palms, papyrus, shaduf, tent, camel, fire pit, water crane, column stumps, awning, jars.

**How borders meet.** No prop crosses a border with a neighbour (the generator fails if one does; a tall prop may rise up to 48 px over a north border only where nothing stands in the zone behind). Every door faces open, reachable ground with nothing standing in front of it (also checked). A road that crosses a border keeps its x (or y) and width and is painted on past the edge, so neither side draws a turf edge there (`paint_road` and the garden's path painter both treat pixels beyond the edge as road). Water, banks and bluffs continue on the same line (the Riverside and the Woodland reuse the garden's stream function with an x offset). Where the ground differs, the side that changes dithers into its neighbour's ground over the last 56 pixels (the Woodland's forest floor into the garden's grass).

**Middle-distance detail (18 Oct).** Open ground between the landmarks gets things at the scale of the companion, each belonging to its place: old pahoehoe flows cooled flat on the ash (rope ridges bowed downstream, lit lip, round toes), fallen basalt scree and charred snags with one crack still glowing in the caldera and the springs; an old gold-foil lander with its short tracks, a natural rock arch you walk through and boulder fields on Lumen; acacias, tamarisk scrub and sandstone outcrops on the oasis dunes. The Skyport's plaza got a still reflecting pool under the platform, a compass rose inlaid in the concourse, a flower kiosk under the guideway and planter beds by the quay. The Woodland now closes in like a forest: the floor goes into dithered deep shade at its outer edges, with more pines, stumps and bushes, and a stone circle stands in its own glade (moss-capped grey-purple stones on a ring of reddish trodden earth, after the reference Matt chose as the bar). Hearthold Castle's yards are used: a slate-hooded well by the bell tower, a rack of practice swords by the dummies, a stone bench behind the keep, banner poles at the bridge and archery butts on the headland meadow. The Ridge is lived on: a woodpile by the cabin, a sled and a snowman on the snow, a dry stone wall across the meadow, cotton grass round the tarn and a marmot on its rock. Inside the hut, the sunroom by the front door has a woven runner, a coat stand, boots on a mat, a lamp table by the window seat and a watering can for the plants. Halcyon's atrium keeps its fish in a standing water column (low gravity), supply crates are strapped down by the shuttle hatch, and the sleep bay has a heap of floor cushions. ## Decorating the hut (C12, 18 Oct)

Matt asked for decorating inside and out, tied to the game's mechanics. The tie is walking: every place the companion walks leaves one keepsake (a brass lantern from town, a bowl of shells from the harbour or the Headland, a cog clock from Cogsworth, a dragon plush from Hearthold, a moon crystal from Lumen; fifteen in all, three of them the hut's own). "Collect once, place freely" (the research in `docs/research/2d-world-design.md`): nothing is bought, nothing wears out, nothing is scored, and moving or putting one away is free and instant.

- The keepsake chest in the bedroom is where it starts: walk to it, DECORATE, choose a keepsake, tap the floor (the companion carries it there), SET IT HERE. Tap one in the room to move it or put it away.
- A keepsake stands on open floor only, keeps room where the companion stands (the door, the bed, the hearth, the table, the sunroom, the chest) and is refused if it would close the way through the room; a rug lies under everything and may carry a keepsake. A save from before a change to the hut sends any keepsake that no longer fits back to the chest.
- Walking into a place for the first time says which keepsake it left. The notice is the only thing that happens; no reward, cost or chore.
- Code: `lib/decor.ts` (catalogue, finding, placement checks, save restore), `scripts/art/decor.py` (the art), `components/decorate-panel.tsx`, the chest in `app/world.tsx`. Tests in `lib/world-save.test.ts`.
- Outside too (18 Oct): IN THE GARDEN in the panel takes you out to the garden still decorating; keepsakes stand on open grass, keep room at every hotspot, the hut's door, where the companion wakes and the four ways out, never on the bridge, and never close a way through. They stand in the valley's view of the garden as well. A keepsake is in one place at a time; setting it out in the other moves it.
- Next: the hut's exterior (door colour, window boxes), which changes the hut's own art rather than adding keepsakes.

## A world that feels alive

All of this is presentation: it never adds rewards, costs or chores, every effect is finite, stops when its screen is inactive, and shows a still frame with reduced motion.

- **Residents with routines (L6).** Each region has its people: Moss and the gardener in the valley, fishers and the lighthouse keeper at the harbour, the castle's steward, tinkerers in Cogsworth, a saloon keeper in Dust Gulch, forge workers in the caldera, street vendors in Neon Harbour, a station botanist on Halcyon. They walk short routes by time of day, greet the companion and say short lines that react to world health. First pass on main (`scripts/art/residents.py`, `lib/resident-routes.ts`): sixteen residents dressed for their places (a gardener and a baker in town, a fisher and the lighthouse keeper, the castle's steward, a tinkerer, a boatwoman on the canal, a cowhand and the saloon keeper, a caravan trader, a smith, a bath attendant, a street vendor and a courier, a porter, the station botanist) walk short routes there and back with pauses, on open ground clear of every prop (checked); greetings and time-of-day routines come with C21.
- **Animals that belong.** Butterflies, fireflies, birds and the frog in the valley; gulls and a harbour cat; ravens on the castle walls; clockwork birds in Cogsworth; lizards and a tumbleweed in Dust Gulch; ember moths in the caldera; neon moths and drones in the city; drifting spores on Lumen. Birds lift off when the companion walks close.
- **Things that move by themselves.** Smoke from chimneys, steam from vents, washing on lines, flags and banners, a windmill, boats bobbing in the harbour, water wheels, lanterns that flicker on at dusk, neon that buzzes on and off, lava that glows and crusts.
- **Weather and light per region (L4).** Rain in Neon Harbour, dust in Dust Gulch, sea mist in the harbour, snow on the ridge; warm windows at night everywhere.
- **The world remembers (L7).** Repaired things stay repaired, befriended residents stay, chosen variants stay.
- **Sound** (`sound.md`): an ambient bed per region (waves, wind, gears, the saloon piano, rain on neon), quiet by default.

**Things move (18 Oct).** Boats, the ferry, the narrowboat and buoys bob a pixel on the water, each on its own phase; the halt's windmill turns, the riverside mill wheel turns and the castle's banners flutter. Turning things are frames drawn by the generator (`windmill_f1`, … exported as `zoneFrames`) and shown one at a time from one slow clock; all of it stops with reduced motion or an inactive screen.

**Windows light up at night (18 Oct).** Every window is drawn by one helper (`garden_props.window`), which records its glass; the zone generator turns those into panes per sprite (`zoneWindows` in `lib/zone-maps.ts`), and after dark the walk lights them warm. A pane with nothing in front glows above the night tint; one that a tree or a nearer house overlaps stays at its building's depth, so it is still hidden behind the tree.

## Battles and customisation per region

- **Battles:** each region has its sparring ring, its resident rival and its backdrop: a harbour jetty, the castle courtyard, a gear-floored arena, a dusty corral, a basalt ring by the lava, a neon rooftop, a zero-g dome. Gentle terrain effects (approved) are balanced by Codex first (C17); none makes raids harsher or costs GRASS. **In the game (18 Oct):** a spar at a region's own ring (Cogsworth, Neon Harbour, the caldera, Lumen) is fought in front of that region: its journey sky and far land and a yard (the near land without the railway, `journeyYards`); the town ring and raids keep the garden's field. Which ring is presentation only (`battlePlace` in `lib/ui.ts`), never a rule.
- **Customisation:** keepsakes from each region become decor at home (a ship in a bottle, a castle banner, a brass clock, a cow skull, an obsidian lamp, a neon sign, a moon rock), and region outfits for the companion (a sailor cap, a crown of daisies, goggles, a cowboy hat, a heat visor, a raincoat with LEDs, a tiny space helmet). Which mission or visit grants which keepsake is a proposal for Matt.
