## The rules

1. **One ground.** Each region's ground is painted in world coordinates from one painter. Biomes (meadow, forest floor, alpine turf, sand) blend over 60 to 100 px, so no change of tone lines up with a tile edge.
2. **Boundaries only for play.** A border closes only where the world itself would stop you: a cliff or height change, water, lava, a private garden, castle walls. Everything else stays open land. A boundary is a real feature, several deep and uneven: tree clumps with overlapping crowns, a ledge, a bank. It is never a painted strip on the seam.
3. **No perfect shapes in nature.** Ponds, clearings, rocks, fields and coasts get organic outlines with banks, rims and contact shadows. Ellipses and rectangles are for things people made.
4. **One camera angle.** Every prop is drawn in the same three-quarter view as the buildings: top faces visible, front faces lit from the upper left, a contact shadow below. No pure side views (carts, airships, trains) or pure top views (parasols, rings) unless the object really lies flat. Long things run east-west where they can. Things that lie flat (rugs, rings, ponds) are foreshortened to the same angle.
5. **Every object has a reason and a footing.** A wall encloses something. A bench faces a view or a path. A washing line stands in a yard, never across a path. A lighthouse stands on rock. A jetty ends at a boat or a ladder. An anchor lies on the quay or hangs from a post.
6. **Paths lead somewhere.** A path never runs into water, a bench or a wall. It ends at a door, a gate, a bridge, a view or another path.
7. **Water is logical.** It has a source and an outlet, and banks of the right material. It is crossed only on a bridge with dry ends. Sea runs on to the horizon or meets land, never forest canopy.
8. **No repeats in a row.** No column or grid of the same prop, unless people made it so: fences, crop rows, a vine line.
9. **Detail at the right density.** No large flat fills. Every surface has its material: bark, shingle, courses, leaf clusters, ground dressing. The Cainos target, within the mobile limits in `art-style.md`.
10. **Life needs what it needs.** Plants and people on the moon or in orbit live inside pressurised, shielded habitats with airlocks, water, light and power. Nothing grows on open regolith, and it never rains on a station.
11. **Detailed from the start.** A new prop or place is drawn to the Cainos village pack's density at once: material, form, light, contact shadow, small story props. "Placeholder now, detail later" is not allowed.
12. **No grids in nature.** Placements read as grown or built for a reason: clusters, paths of use, variation in spacing and size. Nothing falls in rows or columns unless people set it out that way.
13. **World edges.** The camera does not show large areas outside the world. A region's outer edge is a natural limit drawn inside its edge zones (coast, mountains, a forest two or three trees deep). Any empty cell in a region's grid is real scenery that you cannot walk on, drawn with the same care. It is never a filler pattern.

## Checks that catch these

The world lint (`scripts/art/world_lint.py`) reports:

- tone jumps along a tile edge
- long straight lines on a seam
- repeated props in a column
- paths that end in water or against a prop
- large flat areas

Before showing work, look at it at game scale (1 art px = 1.125 screen px), in the browser at 360 px wide, not only as a stitched overview.

### What Eastward's style means for these faults

Matt: "i really really like the artstyle of eastward". From the references in `art-style.md` (warm, lived-in places, light as a material, soft chunky shapes with dark hue-shifted outlines, weather and air) and the scale study in PR #85, for these faults that means:

- **Water moves and has edges.** Highlights are short light dashes that drift with the flow. Foam lies along banks, rocks, piles and the foot of every fall. A fall is a sheet that narrows and breaks into strands, with white water and mist where it lands and a darker, wetter rim of stone round it. Still water reflects what stands over it. (A1, A8, B7, E5, E12)
- **Stone and timber are built, not filled.** Stones are laid in courses of different sizes with mortar or dry joints, chipped corners, moss and damp in the lower joints and a lit coping. Timber shows plank lines, grain, nail heads and worn edges. Bridges have arches, voussoirs, parapets and abutments. Walls have height and a cast shadow. (B2, B6, E13, E15, E16, E20)
- **Animals live.** Every animal has a two to four frame idle loop and a short walk inside the place it belongs to (sheep in their fold, a heron in the shallows, a cat on a doorstep), with a contact shadow, legs and a face drawn in clusters. (A9, B3, B4, B5)
- **The ground is worn where people walk.** Earth shows through grass along desire lines, at gates, doorsteps, benches and the well. Setts are polished at the centre of a square, and puddles collect in hollows at night. Paths widen where people stop and always end somewhere. (E1, E2, E3, E4, E17)
- **Edges are organic.** Ponds, coasts, fields and forest edges wander, with points and coves, banks and overhangs. Forests have a front row of individual trees, saplings and a shaded floor, not a wall of canopy. Only things people made (a quay, a square laid against houses) have straight lines, and even those are chipped and weathered. (A3, A4, A11, B11, E14, E18)
- **Props are dense where people live, and at true scale.** Small story props crowd against walls and in yards (a basket under the washing, pots by the slipway), never in the walking line. Everything is sized to a 25 px resident per the scale table in PR #85. (B1, B8, E8, E10, E11)
- **Light and colour carry the mood.** Warm lamp and window pools against cool blue shadow at night. Colour in the shadows instead of grey. No large flat fills; detail comes in clusters of two or more pixels that stay readable on a phone. (A6, A12)
