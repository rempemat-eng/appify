# The world audit: every cell, one gate

Matt, 20 Oct 20:54: "Can you make absolutely sure the whole map adheres to the highest level of detail, the soft grid etc. specified. not just individual sections but everything." Earlier, at 20:04, on the old summit: "non of this looks natural (given the soft grid), there is no verticality, hard edges".

So the whole world is measured, not samples: `scripts/art/world_audit.py` cuts every region into 32 x 32 px cells and runs every check below on every cell. It rolls the results up per 256 px world tile, zone (or area), region and lane, and it is the gate a region must pass before it counts as rebuilt.

## What it reads

- **Today** (`--source zones`, the default): each region's zones (`lib/zone-maps.ts`: ground, terrain, walk, props, stands), the garden (`assets/garden`, `lib/garden-map.ts`) and every scenery cell past the world (`assets/zones/scenery`), stitched on one canvas per region from `regionLayout`. The residents (`lib/resident-routes.ts`) count as things to meet.
- **Once regions are built on the world canvas** (`--source world`): each chunk's box assembled from its 256 px world tiles (`lib/world-canvas.ts`, `assets/world/**`), with its areas as the places. Both sources go through the same tile iterator, so nothing else changes. Tried on the region-canvas branch (PR #123): Cogsworth, Halcyon Station and the hut read from world tiles give exactly the results they give from their zones, and the isles' four zones the same within one cell (the world canvas carries no scenery yet). A chunk is read with a 32 px ring of the chunk next to it, so its edges are checked for seams too.
- **The standards**, copied onto this branch unchanged (byte for byte the same as their own branches, so the merges are clean): `detail_bar.py` and `detail_baselines.json` (terrace toolkit, PR #90), `style_lint.py`, `house_palette.py` and `art/house-palette.json` (house style, PR #121), `size_check.py` and `size_guide.json` (size guide, PR #124).
- **The asset ledger** (`scripts/art/asset_ledger.json`, claude-a/asset-ledger, PR #142) when it is in the tree, or `--ledger git:REF` to read it from a branch. Without one, every placed asset is "ungraded".

## The checks

A cell is **green** when every check measured in it passes, **amber** on a soft finding (judged by eye, approximate, or waiting on a decision of Matt's), **red** when it fails the standard. A cell with no art is not counted. A check that cannot be measured in a cell (no terrain there, no tier map) leaves it alone; the report says how many cells each check measured.

## Running it

```
python3 scripts/art/world_audit.py                    # the whole world: art/world-audit.json and the images
python3 scripts/art/world_audit.py --region valley    # one region while you work on it (no JSON written)
python3 scripts/art/world_audit.py --cells OUT.json   # every cell's results
python3 scripts/art/world_audit.py --source world     # from the world tiles once they exist
npm run check:world-audit                             # the gate (below)
```

The images go to `napattack-world-audit` in the system's temp folder (never into the repository) unless `--out` or `WORLD_AUDIT_OUT` names a folder: `world-audit.png` (the whole world at half scale, every cell coloured by its worst check, red darker the more checks it fails, zones outlined and labelled with their lane and share), `world-audit-drawing.png` (the same without the asset ledger: how far the drawing itself is from the standard), `regions/<region>.png` (the art at 1x beside its heat map), `regions/<region>-checks.png` (one small map per check) and `worst/` (the ten worst drawing faults at 3x, at most two per check and three per region, with the failing cells outlined). The whole world takes about three minutes on a two-core machine, nearly all of it the detail bar.

`art/world-audit.json` holds the shares per region, lane (by the zones each lane owns; the scenery past the world apart as `A scenery`), prop lane (by the sprites each lane owns), zone, tile and check, each with every check and for the drawing alone (`drawn_*`, the ledger left out), the ten worst problems and the sixty largest with their coordinates (region px, the place's own px and the tile), and every failing sprite. It is written by a whole-world run; regenerate it in the PR that changes a region.

**Where the checks apply.** Built places (the city and Cogsworth) have straight streets, quays and walls by construction, so there `straight-run` holds only main paths to 240 px and `ruler-edge` is off. Interiors (the station's deck and the hut's room) are rooms, not landscape: no `straight-run`, `ruler-edge`, inferred faces or benchmark checks. The scenery past the world has no terrain map, so its coasts, banks and lava edges are read from colour; trees, rocks and snow there are free-form (verticality.md 4f).

**The self-test** (`--selftest`, also run first by the gate) builds a made-up region with known faults (a flat lawn, a staircase bank, a 200 px straight bank off the 8 px lines, two lawns cut at a zone edge, a 20 px face, a tier that ends with no side band) and checks that each check fires on its fault and stays quiet on the right drawing beside it, and that the vectorised bar gives `detail_bar`'s own colour keys and counts on real ground.

## The gate

`art/world-audit-regions.json` marks each region `old` or `rebuilt`. `npm run check:world-audit` audits only the regions marked `rebuilt` and fails unless every one of their cells is green. Today every region is `old`, so it passes without auditing anything; it is not part of `npm run verify` yet.

When a lane rebuilds a region, its PR marks it `rebuilt` and runs the gate. A finding that is right as drawn (a quay that is straight on purpose, with its broken inner edge within 64 px) goes into `exempt` with its rectangle in region px and its reason, as world_lint's overrides do. An exemption is a decision for Claude A or Matt, not for the lane that wants it.

## What it cannot measure yet

- **Tiers on today's world.** No zone has a tier map yet (the terrace toolkit, PR #90, is not in use), so `tier-step`, `north-face` and `side-band` measure nothing today, and face heights are read from bands of wall or cliff in the terrain map. Side bands' 5 px width cannot be read from 4 px terrain cells; it needs the toolkit's pixel kinds.
- **The tier code in world tiles is ambiguous.** `world_canvas.py` and the toolkit run-length encode tiers with a count before each run, so a run of tier digits ("121") cannot be decoded. The audit decodes what it can and says when it cannot; the encoding needs a separator before regions carry tiers.
- **What the terrain map calls a kind.** Paths are read by colour (`world_lint.classify`), so a worn path edge can flicker between path and ground; scenery has no terrain and is classed by colour (water, snow, canopy, ground). A building painted into the ground reads as a wall.
- **Detail on small props.** A cell takes a prop's kind only where one prop covers half of it; small props are measured with the ground round them. Lava, snow, cloth and props with no kind have no baseline and stay amber until there is one.
- **Doors, storeys and footprints inside a sprite** (they need declared `DOOR_RECTS`, `STOREYS` and `FOOT`), stair cheeks and landings, railings at rims, and anything that moves (water flow, residents walking, the night tint).
- **By eye.** Whether a detail makes sense for its object, and whether it looks right at game scale (house-style.md 7), is still a person's judgement. The audit finds where to look.
- **The benchmark's other checks** (borders 64 px deep, something beyond each border, mixing bands, loops, dead ends, ways) need the world plan's routes and are not measured here. Density counts props by their size-guide kind, which is approximate.
