## 1. The 69 retimings at 60 Hz

#257 proposes durations in seconds. A 60 Hz game shows each drawing for a whole number of ticks, so the script turns each proposal into ticks: at least one per drawing, shared out by largest remainder in proportion to #257's numbers, preparation (before the release drawing) and recovery (from it) separately.

| Verdict | Clips | Meaning                                                                                               |
| ------- | ----: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ACCEPT  |    47 | Fits, and the preparation keeps at least 35 % of its drawn time. Use the tick schedule as it is.      |
| HOLD    |    14 | The canonical action is longer than the drawing. Fits; spend the extra on holds, not even stretching. |
| POSE    |     8 | Fits, but the preparation is squeezed below 35 % and will read as a pop. Key poses need a look.       |
| REDRAW  |     0 | No clip has more drawings than ticks, so nothing has to be cut to play at canonical speed.            |

No drawing is shown for less than one tick. The shortest are in the POSE clips (16.7 ms); every ACCEPT and HOLD drawing holds at least 33 ms.

### HOLD (14): hold on the keys

Morel primary and powered_primary; Iona secondary and powered_secondary; Brine primary and powered_primary; Pendle powered_primary; Ashe secondary and powered_secondary; Clack primary, secondary, powered_primary and powered_secondary; Mote special.

Recovery runs 1.17 to 1.8 times the drawn length (Pendle's powered strike 1.8). Mote's special is the one long preparation (1.65 times). Stretching every drawing evenly makes these look slow-motion. The animator's fix, without new drawings:

- **Extra preparation** goes on the anticipation key (the last drawing before release): the wind-up reads as a deliberate hold. This is Mote's case and Clack's.
- **Extra recovery** goes on the first settle drawing after contact (the follow-through hold) and on the last drawing (the settle), roughly two thirds and one third.
- The JSON keeps #257's proportional split so the numbers are reproducible; the hold weighting is a castwork choice for Claude A. If CAST-COMPAT wants it in data, I can add a `--hold-keys` mode that writes it.

### POSE (8): Nix and Rook

Nix primary, secondary, powered_primary and powered_secondary; Rook the same four.

| Clip                      | Release | Prep drawings | Ticks | Prep kept |
| ------------------------- | ------: | ------------: | ----: | --------: |
| Nix primary (+powered)    |   80 ms |         5 (3) |     5 |      24 % |
| Nix secondary (+powered)  |   50 ms |             3 |     3 |      15 % |
| Rook primary (+powered)   |  114 ms |         5 (3) |     7 |      35 % |
| Rook secondary (+powered) |   50 ms |             3 |     3 |      23 % |

These are the quick shots: the canonical release is 50 to 114 ms, so each preparation drawing gets one or two ticks. At 50 ms the eye cannot read three separate drawings; it sees the last one. Recommendation, in order of preference:

1. **Castwork, not timing:** keep one clear anticipation key and drop or merge the in-betweens before release (3 to 1 or 2 drawings), so the key holds 2 to 3 ticks. The recovery is long enough (Nix secondary 1.39 times) to carry the follow-through.
2. If the drawings stay, the tick schedule in the JSON is valid and plays at canonical speed; it just reads as a pop.
3. Never move the release: the release time is the game rule.

Rook primary sits exactly on the threshold (0.346); it is listed for completeness and may be accepted as is.

### ACCEPT (47)

Every other clip. The tick schedules are in the JSON (`ticks`, `release_at`, `ends_at`).
